# Main Power Supply

## 1. Introduction

Electrical power is the backbone of modern medical and diagnostic equipment. In hospitals and radiology departments, electricity is needed for imaging systems, lighting, monitors, computers, air conditioning, heating, sterilization equipment, and many other essential services. A reliable and safe main power supply is therefore critical for the proper functioning of medical devices and for patient and staff safety.

This topic includes the generation, transmission, distribution, and protection of electrical power, along with the basic forms of power supply used in practice. These topics are all connected because electrical power is generated at one place, transformed for efficient transmission, distributed to different points, and finally conditioned and protected before use by equipment.

---

## 2. Generation of electrical energy

Electrical energy is generated mainly by converting mechanical energy into electrical energy using generators.

### 2.1 Principle of generation

The basic principle is electromagnetic induction, discovered by Faraday. When a conductor moves through a magnetic field, or a magnetic field changes around a conductor, an electromotive force (EMF) is induced.

This is the basis of electric generators.

### 2.2 Components of a generator

A generator generally includes:

- rotor (moving part)
- stator (stationary part)
- magnetic field system
- conductors or coils
- prime mover

### 2.3 Prime movers

Mechanical energy is provided by:

- steam turbines
- hydroelectric turbines
- gas turbines
- diesel engines
- wind turbines

These sources rotate the generator shaft, converting mechanical motion into electrical energy.

### 2.4 AC generation

Most large-scale electrical power generation uses alternating current (AC). In AC generation:

- the rotor turns in a magnetic field
- the magnetic flux through the coils changes continuously
- this induces alternating voltage and current

The output is sinusoidal, usually at 50 Hz in many countries or 60 Hz in others.

### 2.5 Why AC is preferred for generation and transmission

Alternating current is preferred because:

- it can be readily transformed to higher or lower voltages
- losses during transmission are reduced at higher voltages
- it is easier to distribute over long distances

This is one of the key reasons AC dominates the electrical grid.

### 2.6 Power stations

Electric power is generated in power stations such as:

- thermal power stations
- hydroelectric stations
- nuclear power stations
- wind farms

The generated voltage is usually relatively low, but it is stepped up for transmission over long distances.

## 3. Distribution of electrical energy

After generation, electrical energy must be transmitted from the power station to the consumer. This is done through a distribution system.

### 3.1 Generation to transmission

The generator output is often at moderate voltage, but for efficient long-distance transmission it is raised by a transformer to a much higher voltage.

### 3.2 Transmission network

Electricity is transmitted through high-voltage transmission lines. High voltage reduces current for the same power, and therefore reduces I²R losses in the conductors.

The basic relationship is:

P = VI

For a given power, increasing voltage decreases current. Lower current means lower heating loss in the cables.

### 3.3 Distribution network

At substations, the voltage is reduced in stages for distribution to homes, hospitals, factories, and clinics.

Typical stages may be:

- generation station: lower voltage
- step-up transformer: high transmission voltage
- substation: reduced voltage for local distribution
- local transformer: final supply voltage to consumers

### 3.4 Why distribution is done in stages

The system is structured in stages to:

- reduce transmission losses
- ensure efficient power flow
- deliver appropriate voltage to end users
- improve safety and reliability

### 3.5 Role of transformers in distribution

Transformers allow voltage to be increased or decreased without changing frequency. They are essential for effective power delivery.

## 4. Generators and transformers

### 4.1 Generators

Generators convert mechanical energy to electrical energy. They form the beginning of the power supply chain. Their output may be AC or DC depending on design.

The output voltage and frequency depend on:

- rotor speed
- number of poles
- magnetic field strength
- coil arrangement

### 4.2 Transformers

A transformer consists of two coils wound around a common magnetic core. It works on the principle of mutual induction.

#### Step-up transformer

- secondary voltage is greater than primary voltage
- used at generation plants for transmission

#### Step-down transformer

- secondary voltage is lower than primary voltage
- used for local distribution and equipment supply

### 4.3 Voltage-current relationship in transformers

For an ideal transformer:

V1 / V2 = N1 / N2

and

P1 ≈ P2

So if voltage increases, current decreases proportionally, which is why high-voltage transmission is efficient.

### 4.4 Importance in hospitals

Transformers are used to provide the required voltages for radiological equipment, operating theatre devices, laboratory equipment, and lighting systems.

## 5. AC and DC power supply with examples

### 5.1 Direct current (DC)

Direct current flows in one direction only.

Examples:

- batteries
- solar cells
- DC power supplies for electronic equipment
- some medical devices and motors

#### Characteristics of DC

- constant polarity
- current direction fixed
- used in electronic circuits and low-voltage applications

### 5.2 Alternating current (AC)

Alternating current changes direction periodically. The voltage and current alternate sinusoidally with time.

Examples:

- household mains power
- power grids
- large hospital power supply systems
- most industrial equipment

#### Characteristics of AC

- voltage and current alternate periodically
- can be transformed easily
- suitable for long-distance transmission

### 5.3 Why AC is used for the main supply

AC is used for the main power system because:

- it is easy to generate
- it can be stepped up and down by transformers
- it reduces transmission losses
- it is suitable for large power networks

### 5.4 DC in medical and electronic equipment

Many electronic devices, imaging systems, and control circuits use DC internally, even though the main power supplied to a hospital is AC. Therefore, AC is converted to DC by rectifiers and power supplies.

### 5.5 Relationship between AC and DC in practice

The main supply may be AC, but internal circuits often need DC for stable operation. This is why medical equipment often has:

- rectifier circuits
- smoothing capacitors
- regulated power supplies
- backup systems

## 6. Single-phase and poly-phase power supply

### 6.1 Single-phase supply

A single-phase system provides one alternating voltage waveform. It is commonly used in residential and light commercial systems.

#### Features

- one alternating voltage
- simple structure
- suitable for small loads
- common for household use

#### Example

Most homes use single-phase 230 V AC or 120 V AC depending on region.

### 6.2 Poly-phase supply

A poly-phase system has more than one AC waveform, usually offset in time. The most common is three-phase power.

#### Three-phase system

Three sinusoidal voltage waves are separated by 120°.

#### Features

- more continuous power delivery
- higher efficiency for large loads
- widely used in industry and large hospitals
- better for motors, transformers, and heavy equipment

### 6.3 Comparison

Single-phase is enough for small loads, but large machines and high-power equipment such as X-ray generators, CT scanners, and MRI systems often use three-phase power because it provides smoother and more efficient power delivery.

### 6.4 Why poly-phase is important in hospitals

Many large medical devices require stable, high-power electrical systems. Three-phase power provides:

- smoother motor performance
- more efficient power transmission
- better load balancing
- reduced flicker and load peaks

This is especially important for heavy equipment in radiology departments.

## 7. Switches, fuses, circuit breakers, and earthing

Electrical systems must be protected and controlled to ensure safety.

### 7.1 Switches

Switches are used to connect or disconnect electrical circuits.

They allow equipment to be turned on or off safely and are used for:

- lighting control
- appliance control
- isolation of equipment
- maintenance work

### 7.2 Fuses

A fuse is a safety device containing a thin wire that melts when the current exceeds a safe value.

#### Purpose

- protect wiring and equipment from excessive current
- prevent fire and equipment damage
- interrupt circuit under fault conditions

#### Limitation

Fuses must be replaced after blowing.

### 7.3 Circuit breakers

A circuit breaker is an automatically operated switch designed to interrupt current under abnormal conditions.

It works by detecting overload or short-circuit conditions and opening the circuit without needing replacement.

#### Advantages over fuses

- reusable
- more convenient
- can be reset after fault correction

### 7.4 Earthing (grounding)

Earthing is a safety method in which the metal casing of equipment is connected to the earth.

#### Purpose

- protect against electric shock
- provide a path for fault current
- reduce risk of dangerous voltage on the equipment casing

#### Why it matters in medicine

Medical equipment may be used near patients, sometimes in wet or conductive conditions. Proper earthing reduces the chance of dangerous leakage currents and protects both staff and patients.

### 7.5 Protective devices in combination

Modern electrical installations usually use:

- switches for control
- fuses or breakers for protection
- earthing for safety
- residual current devices (RCDs) for leakage protection

These work together to ensure reliable and safe power distribution.

## 8. Main voltage drop: causes and remedy

Voltage drop is the reduction in voltage between the source and the point of use. It occurs in all electrical circuits because conductors have resistance and impedance.

### 8.1 Causes of voltage drop

Main causes include:

- long cable length
- small conductor cross-sectional area
- high current load
- poor connections
- corroded terminals
- high resistance joints
- overloaded circuits

### 8.2 Why voltage drop matters

Voltage drop can lead to:

- reduced equipment performance
- overheating of cables
- poor operation of motors and transformers
- unreliable imaging equipment
- dim lights, reduced output, or failure of devices

### 8.3 Formula for voltage drop

The basic relation is:

V = IR

where:

- V = voltage drop
- I = current
- R = resistance

For AC circuits, impedance is used instead of resistance in more exact calculations.

### 8.4 Remedies for voltage drop

- use larger cable cross-sectional area
- reduce cable length where possible
- improve connection quality
- avoid overloading circuits
- use proper distribution design
- use step-up distribution for long distances
- maintain system regularly

### 8.5 Importance in medical facilities

Voltage drop is particularly important in hospitals because equipment such as imaging systems and life-support devices require stable voltage. Fluctuations can compromise operation and patient care.

## 9. Low- and high-tension cables

Cables are categorized according to voltage rating.

### 9.1 Low-tension cables

Low-tension cables are used in low-voltage circuits, such as:

- lighting
- household circuits
- low-voltage distribution
- small equipment

They are usually used for lower operating voltages and are designed for applications where the voltage is relatively safe and controlled.

### 9.2 High-tension cables

High-tension cables carry high voltages and are used mainly in transmission and industrial applications.

They are designed to:

- withstand high electrical stress
- minimize leakage
- resist insulation breakdown
- handle greater power transfer

### 9.3 Insulation and protective design

High-voltage cables require stronger insulation and better shielding than low-voltage cables. The design depends on:

- voltage rating
- expected current
- environmental conditions
- mechanical stress
- heat generation

### 9.4 Why cable type matters

Choosing the wrong cable type can result in:

- insulation failure
- overheating
- power loss
- danger to personnel
- equipment malfunction

### 9.5 Application in hospitals

Hospital power systems use a mix of low-voltage distribution for standard loads and high-voltage distribution for transformers and large equipment. Safe cable routing, insulation, and maintenance are essential in healthcare environments.

## 10. How these topics are related

All the parts of this topic form one continuous electrical system.

### The chain is:

1. Electrical energy is generated at a power station.
2. A generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.
3. A transformer increases voltage for efficient transmission.
4. The power is distributed through transmission and distribution networks.
5. At the consumer end, transformers reduce voltage to the required level.
6. AC or DC supply is used depending on the equipment.
7. Single-phase or three-phase systems are chosen based on the load.
8. Switches, fuses, breakers, and earthing protect the system.
9. Voltage drop must be controlled to maintain stable operation.
10. Cables must be properly selected according to voltage and current rating.

This shows that the supply system is not a collection of isolated topics. It is a complete power chain from generation to final delivery and protection.

## 11. Practical importance in medical physics and hospital settings

In a hospital, power quality and safety are critical because equipment may be life-supporting or diagnostic.

Examples:

- X-ray machines need stable, high-power supplies
- CT and MRI units require reliable three-phase power
- monitors and control systems need regulated DC supplies
- imaging rooms need proper earthing and protection
- backup systems are essential in case of power failure

Thus, the main power supply is not only about electricity delivery; it is directly linked to patient care, equipment reliability, and staff safety.

## 12. Final summary

The main power supply begins with the generation of electrical energy using generators based on electromagnetic induction. The generated power is then transmitted at high voltage to reduce losses and distributed to end users through a network of transformers, substations, and cables. The supply may be AC or DC, and single-phase or multi-phase systems are used according to the application. Protective devices such as switches, fuses, circuit breakers, and earthing are essential for safe operation, while voltage drop and cable selection are critical for reliable supply. High-tension and low-tension cables serve different roles in the distribution system.

These topics are all interrelated because they describe one continuous electrical supply system: generation, transformation, distribution, protection, and safe delivery to the load. In medical environments, this system must be stable, safe, and dependable to support both diagnosis and treatment.
