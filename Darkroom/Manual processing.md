# Photographic Processing: Principles - 1 (Manual Processing)

Photographic processing is the series of chemical steps used to convert a latent image formed in the film into a visible, permanent image. In manual processing, the film is handled step by step in trays or tanks. Each stage depends on the previous one. The quality of the final radiograph or photograph depends not only on exposure, but also on the correct chemical action, timing, temperature, and agitation during development, fixing, washing, and drying.

## 1. Acidity, Alkalinity and pH

The chemical behavior of processing solutions is strongly controlled by their acidity or alkalinity. This is measured by pH.

- pH is a scale from 0 to 14.
- A pH of 7 is neutral.
- Values below 7 are acidic.
- Values above 7 are alkaline (basic).

### What do acidity and alkalinity mean?

- An acidic solution contains more hydrogen ions ($H^+$) and tends to neutralize alkalis.
- An alkaline solution contains more hydroxide ions ($OH^-$) and tends to neutralize acids.

### Why is pH important in processing?

pH controls how quickly and effectively chemical reactions occur.

- In developer solution, the correct alkaline pH helps the developing agents to reduce exposed silver halide crystals into metallic silver.
- In fixer solution, an acidic pH helps stop development and removes unexposed silver halides safely.
- If the pH is too low or too high, the reaction may become too slow, too fast, or chemically unbalanced.

### Relationship between pH and processing quality

A correct pH ensures:

- proper development speed,
- good contrast,
- minimal fog,
- clean fixation,
- stable image quality.

If pH is incorrect, the image may become weak, cloudy, foggy, or poorly fixed.

## 2. Development

Development is the first and most important stage. Its purpose is to make the invisible latent image visible by converting exposed silver halide crystals into black metallic silver.

### What happens during development?

When the film is exposed to light or x-rays, a latent image is formed in the emulsion. The developer acts on these exposed crystals and reduces them to metallic silver. The more exposure a crystal receives, the more easily it is reduced. This gives the film its tonal differences.

### Developer solution

The developer is usually a complex solution containing several ingredients, each with a specific role:

1. Developing agents
   - These are the main chemicals that reduce exposed silver halide to metallic silver.
   - Examples include hydroquinone and metol (or phenidone in modern formulations).
   - They are responsible for image formation.

2. Activator (alkali)
   - Usually sodium carbonate or similar alkali.
   - It raises the pH of the solution so that the developing agents can work efficiently.
   - Without the correct alkaline conditions, development would be very slow.

3. Preservative
   - Usually sodium sulfite.
   - It protects the developer from oxidation by air.
   - Oxidation would weaken the solution and lower its effectiveness.

4. Restrainer
   - Usually potassium bromide.
   - It slows development in unexposed areas and helps control fog.
   - It prevents the developer from acting too strongly on the whole film.

5. Solvent (water)
   - Water dissolves the chemicals and provides the medium for the reaction.

### Developer activity

Developer activity means how strongly and how quickly the developer works. It is influenced by:

- temperature,
- concentration,
- pH,
- agitation,
- age of the solution.

### Effect of temperature

- Higher temperature increases chemical activity and speeds development.
- Lower temperature slows development.

If the temperature is too high, the film may become overdeveloped, contrast may increase, and fog may appear. If it is too low, development becomes weak, the image may be thin and low in contrast.

### Effect of time

Development time must be controlled carefully. Too long means overdevelopment; too short means underdevelopment.

### Effect of agitation

Agitation helps fresh developer reach the film surface. Without agitation, the developer near the emulsion becomes exhausted and development becomes uneven.

### Role of development in image formation

Development determines:

- density,
- contrast,
- detail visibility,
- overall image quality.

This stage is the foundation of the final image. If development is poor, later stages cannot fully correct the result.

## 3. Fixing

After development, the film still contains undeveloped silver halide crystals. These must be removed or the image will remain light-sensitive and eventually darken. This is the purpose of fixing.

### What happens during fixing?

The fixer dissolves the unexposed and undeveloped silver halide crystals that remain in the emulsion. Only the developed silver image remains.

### Fixing solution

The fixing solution usually contains:

1. Fixing agent
   - Usually sodium thiosulfate (hypo).
   - This chemical removes the remaining silver halide from the film.

2. Acidifier
   - Often acetic acid.
   - It stops the action of the developer and helps preserve the image.

3. Preservative
   - Prevents decomposition of the fixing solution.

4. Hardener
   - Sometimes added to strengthen the gelatin layer and reduce swelling.

### Fixing activity

Fixing activity depends on:

- concentration of the fixer,
- time in the solution,
- temperature,
- condition of the fixer.

A weak or exhausted fixer may leave residual silver halide, causing the film to appear milky or to fade over time.

### Importance of proper fixing

Proper fixing ensures:

- the image becomes permanent,
- the film is no longer sensitive to light,
- the final image remains stable.

If the film is not fixed properly, it may turn brown, foggy, or show poor archival quality.

---

## 4. Washing and Drying

Once fixing is complete, the film still contains residual chemicals from the developer and fixer. These must be removed by washing and then the film must be dried properly.

### Washing

Washing removes leftover fixer and processing chemicals from the emulsion. If these chemicals remain, they can cause:

- staining,
- fading,
- poor image stability,
- tacky or sticky surfaces.

The purpose of washing is to make the image permanent and chemically stable.

### Why washing is important

The washing stage protects the image from deterioration. Residual fixer, especially thiosulfate, can slowly react with the silver image and cause discoloration over time.

### Drying

After washing, the film is dried to remove water. Drying must be done carefully to avoid:

- water spots,
- streaks,
- scratches,
- distortion or swelling of the emulsion.

A properly dried film is clean, stable, and ready for viewing or storage.

---

## 5. How These Processes Are Related

These stages are closely connected. They are not separate events; they form a chain of chemical reactions.

### Relationship between development and pH

Development needs an alkaline environment. The developer solution works efficiently only at the correct pH. If the pH is too low, development becomes weak and slow. If it is too high, the reaction may become too aggressive, causing fog and poor contrast.

### Relationship between development and fixing

Development creates the visible silver image, but it leaves behind unexposed silver halide. Fixing removes these remaining crystals so that only the desired image remains. Therefore, development creates the image and fixing preserves it.

### Relationship between fixing and washing

Fixing is only complete if the film is adequately washed afterward. If fixing chemicals remain in the film, the image may not be stable. Washing removes the chemical residues introduced during fixing.

### Relationship between washing and drying

Washing prepares the film for final drying. If washing is incomplete, drying may trap residual chemicals and cause staining. Proper drying completes the processing cycle and preserves the final image.

### Summary of the whole process

The photographic processing sequence may be seen as follows:

1. Development makes the latent image visible.
2. The developer works best at the right pH and temperature.
3. Fixing removes the remaining unexposed silver halide.
4. Washing removes residual chemicals from the film.
5. Drying preserves the final image in a clean and stable form.

In simple terms, development forms the image, fixing makes it permanent, washing cleans it, and drying prepares it for storage or use.

---

## 6. Key Points to Remember

- pH controls the behavior of processing chemicals.
- Development depends on an alkaline environment and proper activity.
- Fixing depends on the chemical removal of unexposed silver halide.
- Washing removes leftover chemicals and protects image permanence.
- Drying completes the process and prevents damage to the film.

Understanding these principles helps in producing clear, permanent, and high-quality images in manual processing.
