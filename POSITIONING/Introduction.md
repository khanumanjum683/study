# Radiographic Positioning and Techniques

Your question paper mostly follows this pattern:

- Upper limb
- Lower limb

## 2. Basic terms you must know

### A. Anatomical terms

| Term | Meaning | Easy example |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior | Front side | Chest is anterior |
| Posterior | Back side | Spine is posterior |
| Superior | Upper side | Head is superior to chest |
| Inferior | Lower side | Feet are inferior to knee |
| Medial | Near midline | Nose is medial |
| Lateral | Away from midline | Ear is lateral |
| Proximal | Near trunk | Shoulder is proximal to hand |
| Distal | Away from trunk | Fingers are distal |
| Supine | Lying on back | AP abdomen |
| Prone | Lying on front | PA chest |
| Lateral | Side lying | Lateral skull |
| Oblique | Slanted position | Oblique hand |

### B. Body planes

```text
             Superior
                |
      -----------------
      |  Coronal     |
      |  Front/Back  |
      -----------------
                |
      Sagittal = left/right
      Transverse = upper/lower
             Inferior
```

- Coronal plane: divides body into front and back
- Sagittal plane: divides body into left and right
- Transverse plane: divides body into upper and lower

## 3. Positioning and projection terminology

### A. Basic definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Position | How the patient is placed |
| Projection | Direction of X-ray beam |
| View | What body part is seen on film |
| AP | X-ray passes from front to back |
| PA | X-ray passes from back to front |
| Lateral | Side view |
| Oblique | Slanting view |
| Decubitus | Patient lying down; beam horizontal |

### Diagram: AP vs PA vs Lateral

```text
AP projection:
Front -> X-ray beam -> Back -> Film

PA projection:
Back -> X-ray beam -> Front -> Film

Lateral projection:
Side -> X-ray beam -> Opposite side -> Film
```

> In exam: always write patient position + part position + central ray + cassette placement.

## 4. How to answer positioning questions for high marks

Use this 5-point formula:

1. Patient position
2. Part position
3. Central ray direction
4. Film/cassette placement
5. Structures shown / purpose

### Example answer format

- Patient: supine
- Part: arm abducted 90°
- Central ray: perpendicular to shoulder joint
- Film: 10 × 12 cassette
- Structures shown: shoulder joint and surrounding bones

This is the type of answer examiners like.

## 5. Skull and facial bones

### A. Lateral skull
- Patient: lateral position
- Part: side of head touching cassette
- Central ray: perpendicular to 2.5 cm above external auditory meatus
- Structures seen: skull bones, sutures, sella turcica, paranasal sinuses

### B. PA skull
- Patient: prone or forehead-nose against film
- Central ray: perpendicular to nasion
- Structures seen: frontal bone, orbital rims, cranium
- Used for: frontal bone and cranial vault

### C. Caldwell view
- PA skull with 15° caudal angulation
- Best for: frontal sinuses and orbital floor
- Exam key: “Caldwell view shows frontal sinus clearly”

### D. Waters view
- Patient: chin raised, nose against film
- Best for: maxillary sinus
- Common exam question: “Explain the projection for maxillary sinus”

### E. Townes view
- AP skull with 30° caudal angle
- Best for: occipital bone and foramen magnum

### F. Sella turcica view
- Lateral skull or special view
- Used for pituitary region

### Very easy answer for skull
“Skull radiography includes AP/PA, lateral and special views such as Caldwell, Waters and Townes. These views are used to demonstrate cranial bones, sinuses, sella turcica and base of skull according to the area to be studied.”

## 6. Facial bones and sinuses

### Important views

| View | Best for |
|---|---|
| Waters view | Maxillary sinus |
| Caldwell view | Frontal sinus |
| Lateral sinus view | Sphenoid and maxillary sinus |
| Occipitomental view | Facial bones |

### Easy exam note
- Maxillary sinus = Waters view
- Frontal sinus = Caldwell view
- Sphenoid sinus = lateral view
- Facial bones = occipitomental view

## 7. Vertebral column

### A. Cervical spine
- AP open mouth: odontoid process
- Lateral cervical: vertebral bodies, intervertebral spaces
- Swimmer’s view: lower cervical and upper thoracic junction
- Flexion-extension views: instability

### B. Thoracic spine
- AP and lateral views
- Shows vertebral bodies, intervertebral spaces and spinous processes

### C. Lumbar spine
- AP, lateral and oblique views
- Best seen in lateral view for disc spaces and vertebral bodies
- Spot view for pars interarticularis

### D. Scoliosis and deformities
- AP full spine
- Used to assess spinal curvature

### Exam-friendly answer
“Cervical spine is examined in AP open mouth, lateral and swimmer’s view. Thoracic and lumbar vertebrae are examined in AP and lateral views to study vertebral bodies, intervertebral spaces, fractures and deformities.”

## 8. Upper limb positioning

### A. Shoulder
- AP shoulder
- Axial shoulder
- Lateral shoulder
- Scapular Y view
- Used for dislocation and fractures

### B. Clavicle
- AP clavicle
- Used to see clavicle fracture

### C. Humerus
- AP and lateral humerus
- Used to assess shaft fracture

### D. Elbow
- AP and lateral elbow
- Important for joint space, radial head and olecranon

### E. Forearm
- AP and lateral forearm

### F. Wrist
- PA, lateral, oblique
- Carpal tunnel view: special view for carpal tunnel
- Scaphoid view: for scaphoid fracture

### G. Hand
- PA hand
- Oblique hand
- Lateral hand

### Carpal tunnel projection
- Patient: wrist in neutral position
- Beam: angled 25–30° caudal
- Film: 10 × 12
- Purpose: visualize carpal tunnel and hamulus

## 9. Lower limb and pelvis

### A. Hip
- AP hip
- Lateral hip
- Frog leg lateral
- Used for fracture and dislocation

### B. Pelvis
- AP pelvis
- Used to see sacroiliac joints, symphysis pubis and femoral heads

### C. Femur
- AP and lateral femur

### D. Knee
- AP knee
- Lateral knee
- Intercondylar fossa view
- Patellar skyline view

### E. Tibia-fibula
- AP and lateral

### F. Ankle
- AP, lateral, oblique
- Mortise view

### G. Foot
- AP foot, lateral foot and oblique foot

### Easy exam note
“Hip and pelvis are imaged in AP and lateral views. Knee is evaluated in AP, lateral and intercondylar fossa view. Foot and ankle are studied in AP, lateral and oblique views to identify fractures and deformities.”

## 11. Chest and thorax

### A. PA chest
- Best view for heart and lungs
- Patient: erect, chest against film
- Central ray: at T6
- Used for: lungs, heart and mediastinum

### B. Lateral chest
- Side view to see heart and lungs
- Helps in lesion localization

### C. AP chest
- Used in critically ill or bedridden patient

### D. Lordotic view
- Used to visualize apices of lungs

## 12. Most repeated short answer topics

### 1. What is contrast media?
- It is a substance used to improve visibility of body parts in X-ray or imaging.
- Positive contrast: barium, iodine
- Negative contrast: air

### 2. What is projection?
- It is the direction of the X-ray beam from tube to patient and film.

### 3. What is position?
- It means how the patient is placed for the examination.

### 4. What is lateral view?
- Side view of the body part.

### 5. What is oblique view?
- Body part is rotated at an angle.

### 6. Difference between AP and PA
- AP: beam enters front and exits back
- PA: beam enters back and exits front

## 13. Very important 5-mark answer patterns

### Example 1: Explain the projection for carpal tunnel
- Place patient in prone or seated position
- Forearm supported
- Wrist hyperextended
- Central ray directed 25–30° caudal through carpal tunnel
- Purpose: show median nerve compression and carpal tunnel bones

### Example 2: Explain the projection for shoulder joint
- Patient: AP position
- Arm slightly abducted
- External rotation of humerus
- Central ray to shoulder joint
- Film placed against shoulder
- Used to assess glenohumeral joint and surrounding structures

### Example 3: Explain Waters view
- Patient in prone with chin raised
- Nose touching cassette
- Central ray perpendicular to acanthion
- Shows maxillary sinus clearly
- Best view for maxillary sinus pathology

### Example 4: Explain lateral skull
- Patient in lateral position
- Interpupillary line perpendicular to film
- Central ray at 2.5 cm above external auditory meatus
- Shows skull, sella turcica and cranial base

## 16. Final exam strategy: how to score more

### Write like this in exam:
- “Patient position: supine/prone/lateral”
- “Part position: rotated/abducted/flexed”
- “Central ray: perpendicular/caudal/cephalic”
- “Film: AP/PA/lateral”
- “Structures seen: …”
- “Indications: …”

This structure is simple and marks-friendly.

## 17. One-line revision summary

- AP = front to back
- PA = back to front
- Lateral = side view
- Oblique = slanting view
- Projection = beam direction
- Position = patient placement
- Always mention what is seen and why it is taken

## 19. Short answer ready version

“Radiographic positioning means placing the patient correctly so that the desired body part is visualized clearly. Projection is the direction of X-ray beam. AP and PA are common projections used in chest, abdomen and extremities. Lateral and oblique views help to assess structures not seen in the standard view. Correct positioning is essential for good radiographic image quality and proper diagnosis.”
