# Femur Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Femur radiography is commonly asked in radiographic positioning. The important topics are:
- Related radiological anatomy
- Mid and distal femur: AP and lateral views
- Mid and proximal femur: AP and lateral views

Exam answer should include:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Central ray
- Structures shown
- Important points

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of femur
The femur is the longest bone of the body.

It is divided into:
- Upper end: head, neck, greater trochanter, lesser trochanter
- Shaft
- Lower end: medial and lateral condyles, patellar surface, intercondylar fossa

Important landmarks:
- Femoral head
- Neck
- Greater trochanter
- Lesser trochanter
- Medial and lateral condyles
- Shaft of femur

These landmarks are important in AP and lateral views.

---

## 3. Mid and distal femur: AP view

### Indication
- Fracture of shaft of femur
- Tumor
- Infection
- Pathology of mid and distal femur

### Position of patient
- Patient lies supine
- Lower limb is extended and rotated internally slightly
- Leg is placed in true AP position
- The femur is centered to the cassette

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the femur
- Centered at the midshaft or distal femur depending on the area required

### Structures shown
- Shaft of femur
- Distal femur
- Condyles
- Patellar surface
- Soft tissue around the bone

### Key points
- This view is used for fractures of shaft and distal femur
- Both knee joint and lower femur are included in the radiograph if possible

### Diagram
```text
AP femur
Patient supine
Leg extended
Femur centered to cassette
     Shaft of femur
     Distal femur
     Knee joint region
```

### Exam answer
"AP view of the mid and distal femur is used to assess fracture and bone pathology of the shaft and distal femur. The patient is positioned supine with the lower limb extended and slightly internally rotated. The central ray is directed to the midshaft or distal femur. This view shows the shaft, distal femur, condyles and knee joint region."

---

## 4. Mid and distal femur: Lateral view

### Indication
- Fracture of shaft or distal femur
- Deformity
- Localized bone pathology
- Alignment assessment

### Position of patient
- Patient lies on the side
- Affected limb is placed lateral to the cassette
- Knee and hip are flexed slightly
- The femur is aligned with the long axis of the cassette

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the femur
- Centered at the midshaft or distal femur

### Structures shown
- Distal femur
- Condyles
- Patella
- Soft tissue outline
- Bone alignment

### Key points
- Lateral view helps in checking fracture alignment and displacement
- It gives better view of the posterior structures of the femur

### Diagram
```text
Lateral femur
Patient lateral
Affected limb placed on side
    Distal femur
    Condyles
    Patella
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral view of the mid and distal femur is performed to assess fracture displacement and alignment. The patient is placed in lateral position with the affected femur against the cassette. The central ray is directed to the midshaft or distal femur. This view shows the distal femur, condyles and patella clearly in lateral relationship."

---

## 5. Mid and proximal femur: AP view

### Indication
- Fracture of proximal femur
- Neck of femur fracture
- Greater trochanter pathology
- Hip joint disease

### Position of patient
- Patient lies supine
- Leg is internally rotated 15–20°
- Hip and knee are extended
- Femoral neck is centered to the cassette

### Central ray
- Directed to the femoral neck or proximal femur
- Perpendicular to the hip region

### Structures shown
- Femoral head
- Neck of femur
- Greater trochanter
- Lesser trochanter
- Proximal femoral shaft

### Key points
- This view is important to assess the hip and upper femur
- It helps in fracture of femoral neck and upper femur

### Diagram
```text
AP proximal femur
Patient supine
Leg internally rotated
  Head of femur
  Neck of femur
  Greater trochanter
```

### Exam answer
"AP view of the mid and proximal femur is used to examine the upper femur and hip region. The patient is supine with the lower limb internally rotated. The central ray is directed to the femoral neck or proximal femur. This view shows the femoral head, neck, greater trochanter and proximal shaft."

---

## 6. Mid and proximal femur: Lateral view

### Indication
- Fracture of proximal femur or neck
- Hip joint problem
- Abnormality of greater trochanter

### Position of patient
- Patient lies lateral
- Affected hip is placed close to the cassette
- The femur is slightly abducted or rotated depending on technique

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the proximal femur
- Centered to the femoral neck or upper femur

### Structures shown
- Femoral neck
- Greater and lesser trochanter
- Shaft and upper femur
- Hip joint relationship

### Key points
- Lateral view is useful for fracture alignment and hip joint visualization
- It helps in many proximal femoral injuries

### Diagram
```text
Lateral proximal femur
Patient lateral
Affected hip near cassette
   Greater trochanter
   Neck of femur
   Upper shaft
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral view of the proximal femur is used to assess proximal femoral alignment and fracture. The patient is placed in lateral position with the affected hip close to the cassette. The central ray is directed to the upper femur. This view demonstrates the neck of femur, greater trochanter and upper shaft better."

---

## 7. Comparison table

| View | Area | Main use |
|---|---|---|
| AP femur | Mid and distal femur | Shaft fracture, bone pathology |
| Lateral femur | Mid and distal femur | Alignment, fracture displacement |
| AP proximal femur | Upper femur and hip | Neck fracture, trochanteric pathology |
| Lateral proximal femur | Upper femur and hip | Alignment and hip region assessment |

---

## 8. Short revision notes
- AP femur = supine, leg extended, slight internal rotation
- Lateral femur = lateral position, fracture alignment
- Proximal femur AP = internal rotation to show neck clearly
- Proximal femur lateral = lateral view for upper femur and hip

---

## 9. Final easy 5-mark answer
"Femur radiography is divided into AP and lateral projections of the mid and distal femur and the mid and proximal femur. In AP mid and distal femur, the patient is supine with the limb extended and slightly internally rotated. The central ray is directed to the midshaft or distal femur. This view shows the shaft, distal femur and knee joint. In lateral femur, the patient is in lateral position and the affected femur is placed close to the cassette. This helps to assess fracture alignment and displacement. For proximal femur, AP view is taken with internal rotation to visualize the femoral neck and trochanter clearly. The lateral view of the proximal femur is used to assess the upper femur and hip region."

---

## 10. Memory trick
- AP femur = patient supine, leg straight
- Lateral femur = patient lateral, fracture alignment
- Proximal femur = look at femoral neck and trochanter

This topic is easy if you remember the position and the specific part of the femur being examined.
