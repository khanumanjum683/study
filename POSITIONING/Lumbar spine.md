# Lumbar Spine Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Lumbar spine is one of the most important chapters in radiographic positioning. The common views are:
- AP lumbar spine
- Lateral lumbar spine
- Oblique lumbar spine
- AP axial (L5-S1)
- Lateral (L5-S1)
- Scoliosis series
- Flexion and extension views
- Spot view for pars interarticularis

The exam usually asks:
- Patient position
- Central ray direction
- Structures shown
- Why the view is taken

---

## 2. Related radiographic anatomy
The lumbar spine consists of 5 vertebrae: L1 to L5.

Important structures:
- Vertebral bodies
- Intervertebral disc spaces
- Pedicles
- Laminae
- Spinous processes
- Transverse processes
- Pars interarticularis
- Zygapophyseal joints
- Sacrum and coccyx region (for L5-S1 view)

### Simple anatomy diagram
```text
      Cervical spine   Thoracic spine   Lumbar spine   Sacrum
          |                 |               |             |
          C1-C7            T1-T12          L1-L5        S1

        AP lumbar spine shows vertebral bodies and disc spaces
        Lateral lumbar spine shows alignment and disc spaces
        Oblique view shows zygapophyseal joints
```

## 3. AP lumbar spine view

### Indication
- Low back pain
- Fracture
- Infection
- Tumor
- Scoliosis
- Degenerative disease

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or erect
- The midline of the lumbar spine is centered to the cassette
- Legs may be flexed to reduce lumbar lordosis in supine position

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered to the level of L3 or the iliac crest

### Structures shown
- Lumbar vertebral bodies
- Intervertebral disc spaces
- Spinous processes
- Transverse processes
- Sacrum and coccyx may be included if necessary

### Key points
- AP view is a routine view for lumbar spine
- It gives a good general survey of vertebral bodies and disc spaces
- It may be used in scoliosis evaluation

### Simple diagram
```text
      X-ray tube
          |
          V
     Patient in AP position
         Lumbar spine centered
           CR at L3/iliac crest
   Vertebral bodies and disc spaces seen
```

### Exam answer
"AP lumbar spine view is taken to demonstrate the vertebral bodies, intervertebral spaces and spinous processes of the lumbar vertebrae. The patient is placed in supine or erect position with the lumbar spine centered to the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the cassette at the level of L3 or iliac crest. This view is used to study fractures, degenerative changes and general lumbar pathology."

---

## 4. Lateral lumbar spine view

### Indication
- Low back pain
- Disc disease
- Compression fracture
- Spondylolisthesis
- Deformity
- Tumor

### Patient position
- Patient is in true lateral position
- Side of the body against the cassette
- The lumbar spine is parallel to the film
- Knees may be flexed for comfort

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered at L3 or the iliac crest level

### Structures shown
- Vertebral bodies
- Intervertebral disc spaces
- Spinous processes
- Lumbosacral junction
- Alignment of the vertebrae

### Key points
- Lateral lumbar spine is the best view for disc spaces and vertebral alignment
- It is useful in suspected fracture and spondylolisthesis
- It also shows the lumbosacral junction

### Diagram
```text
      X-ray tube
          |
          V
    Patient in true lateral position
      Lumbar spine parallel to film
      CR at L3/iliac crest
    Lateral view shows disc spaces clearly
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral lumbar spine view is the most important projection for studying the intervertebral disc spaces and vertebral alignment. The patient is positioned in true lateral with the lumbar spine against the film and centered to the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the cassette at the level of L3 or iliac crest. This view is particularly useful in identifying disc disease, fractures and spondylolisthesis."

---

## 5. Oblique lumbar spine view

### Indication
- Facet joint pathology
- Spondylolisthesis
- Low back pain
- Zygapophyseal joint evaluation

### Patient position
- Patient is in oblique position
- Body rotated 45°
- Side of interest is placed near the cassette

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered to the lumbar spine at L3 level

### Structures shown
- Zygapophyseal joints
- Facet joints
- Pars interarticularis
- Pedicles

### Key points
- Oblique view is best to see zygapophyseal joints
- It is used to assess facet joint disease
- It is also helpful in detecting pars fracture

### Diagram
```text
      Patient rotated 45°
      Side of interest near film
        shows zygapophyseal joints
             Oblique view
```

### Exam answer
"Oblique lumbar spine view is taken to visualize the zygapophyseal joints and pars interarticularis. The patient is rotated 45° from the lateral position and the side of interest is placed near the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the cassette at L3 level. This view is useful for assessing facet joint pathology and spondylolisthesis."

---

## 6. AP axial view (L5-S1)

### Indication
- Lumbosacral junction pathology
- Spondylolisthesis
- Sacroiliac region evaluation
- Low back pain

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or prone
- The plane of the lumbosacral region is centered to the cassette

### Central ray
- Directed 30° to 35° cephalad for AP axial projection
- Centered to the pubic symphysis or lumbosacral junction

### Structures shown
- L5-S1 joint space
- Lumbosacral junction
- Sacrum and adjacent vertebrae

### Key points
- This view helps to see L5-S1 clearly
- It is important in lumbosacral pathology

### Exam answer
"AP axial projection of the lumbosacral spine is taken to demonstrate the lumbosacral junction and L5-S1 joint space. The patient is positioned in supine or prone with the lumbosacral area centered to the film. The central ray is directed 30° to 35° cephalad towards the pubic symphysis. This view is useful in suspected spondylolisthesis and lumbosacral pathology."

---

## 7. Lateral view (L5-S1)

### Indication
- Lumbosacral junction disease
- Fracture
- Disc lesion
- Spondylolisthesis

### Patient position
- Patient is in true lateral position
- The lumbosacral junction is centered to the cassette

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the lumbosacral region
- Usually angled slightly or centered to L5-S1 level

### Structures shown
- L5-S1 joint space
- Vertebral alignment
- Disc spaces at junction

### Key points
- This view is important for lumbosacral joint study
- It shows the joint space clearly

---

## 8. Scoliosis series

### Indication
- Scoliosis
- Kyphosis
- Postural deformity
- Spinal curvature study

### Views included
- AP standing full spine
- Erect lateral spine
- Sometimes supine and prone views

### Structures shown
- Whole spine
- Curvature and deformity
- Vertebral rotation

### Key points
- Scoliosis series is done to study whole spine deformity
- AP erect position is commonly used

### Exam answer
"Scoliosis series is a special radiographic examination of the whole spine used to assess spinal curvature and deformity. In the standard series, AP standing full spine is done and sometimes lateral view is added. This helps to evaluate scoliosis, kyphosis and vertebral rotation."

---

## 9. AP or PA bending views

### Indication
- Scoliosis assessment
- Functional curve evaluation
- Postural deformity

### Patient position
- Patient stands with bending to right or left
- AP or PA projection is used

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette

### Structures shown
- Structural versus functional scoliosis
- Degree of curvature

### Key points
- Bending views help to differentiate fixed curve from flexible curve

---

## 10. Flexion and extension views

### Indication
- Spondylolisthesis
- Instability
- Low back pain with motion study

### Patient position
- Patient in lateral position
- Flexion: patient bends forward
- Extension: patient bends backward

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the lumbar spine

### Structures shown
- Movement of vertebrae
- Stability of lumbar spine

### Key points
- These views help to study instability of the vertebrae

---

## 11. Sacrum and coccyx

### A. AP axial sacrum
- Indication: sacral pathology
- Patient: supine
- CR: 15° cephalad
- Shows: sacrum

### B. AP axial coccyx
- Indication: coccygeal pain
- Patient: supine
- CR: 10° caudad
- Shows: coccyx

### C. Lateral sacrum
- Shows: sacrum in profile

### D. Lateral coccyx
- Shows: coccyx in profile

### Key exam point
- Sacrum and coccyx are seen in special axial and lateral projections

## 14. Easy 5-mark answer
"Lumbar spine radiography includes AP, lateral and oblique views. The AP lumbar spine view is taken with the patient in supine or erect position and the central ray directed to L3 or iliac crest. It demonstrates the vertebral bodies, intervertebral spaces and spinous processes. The lateral lumbar spine view is the most important projection for disc spaces and vertebral alignment. The oblique view is taken at 45° rotation to visualize the zygapophyseal joints. In addition, special views like AP axial L5-S1 and lateral L5-S1 are used to evaluate the lumbosacral junction and sacroiliac region."

---

## 15. Final revision summary
Lumbar spine is usually examined by AP, lateral and oblique views. The AP view shows vertebral bodies, lateral shows disc spaces and alignment, and oblique shows facet joints. Special views like L5-S1 and scoliosis series are used in certain conditions. If you remember patient position, central ray and structures shown, you can write high-scoring answers.
