# Shoulder Girdle: Basic and Special Projections

## 1. Simple meaning

Shoulder girdle is the bony ring formed by:
- clavicle
- scapula
- humeral head
- sternoclavicular joint
- acromioclavicular joint

It helps in the movement of the shoulder and is frequently examined for fractures, dislocations and degenerative changes.

## 3. Shoulder non-trauma routine

These are routine views taken when there is no fracture or major trauma.

| Projection | Position | Purpose | Key point |
|---|---|---|---|
| AP external rotation | Patient supine or erect, arm externally rotated | Shows greater tubercle and shoulder joint | Humerus externally rotated to show greater tubercle clearly |
| AP internal rotation | Arm internally rotated | Shows lesser tubercle and humeral head better | Humerus rotated medially |
| Superior-inferior axial | Patient seated or supine; arm abducted | Shows shoulder joint and intertubercular groove | Beam directed superior to inferior |
| Infero-superior axial (Lawrence method) | Arm abducted 90°, hand supinated | Best view for glenohumeral joint | Common for dislocation and joint space |
| Infero-superior shoulder projection (West Point method) | Arm abducted, patient prone or supine | Shows glenoid cavity and anterior structures | Useful for anterior dislocation |
| Posterior oblique (Glenoid cavity / Grashley method) | Patient oblique position | Shows glenoid cavity without overlap | Useful for glenoid fracture or pathology |
| Intertubercular groove (Fisk method) | Patient supine or erect, arm rotated | Shows bicipital groove | Best for bicipital tendinitis |

### A. AP external rotation
- Patient is erect or supine
- Arm is externally rotated
- Central ray: perpendicular to shoulder joint
- Purpose: shows greater tubercle and shoulder joint
- Important for: shoulder anatomy and fracture assessment

### B. AP internal rotation
- Arm is internally rotated
- Humerus rotated medially
- Purpose: better demonstration of lesser tubercle and humeral head

### C. Superior-inferior axial view
- Beam passes from superior to inferior
- Arm is abducted
- Used to show shoulder joint and surrounding structures

### D. Infero-superior axial (Lawrence method)
- Patient stands or sits with arm abducted 90°
- Hand is supinated
- Beam directed from inferior to superior
- Very important view for glenohumeral joint and dislocations

### E. West Point projection
- Special projection for glenoid cavity and anterior shoulder structures
- Useful in shoulder dislocation cases

### F. Grashley method (posterior oblique)
- Patient in oblique position
- Shows glenoid cavity clearly without overlapping structures
- Used for glenoid pathology and fracture

### G. Fisk method (intertubercular groove)
- Used to see bicipital groove
- Helps in identifying long head of biceps tendon pathology

## 4. Shoulder trauma routine

These views are taken when there is trauma, fracture, or shoulder injury.

| Projection | Purpose | Common use |
|---|---|---|
| AP neutral rotation | Shows fracture without rotation | Initial trauma view |
| Transthoracic lateral (Lawrence method) | Shows shoulder and upper humerus when arm cannot move | Fracture of proximal humerus |
| Tangential projection – supraspinatus outlet (Neer method) | Shows outlet of supraspinatus and acromion | Impingement syndrome |
| Apical oblique projection (Garth method) | Shows shoulder dislocation and posterior dislocation | Special trauma view |

### A. AP neutral rotation
- Arm is in neutral position
- Patient is erect or supine
- Important in trauma because it avoids movement of injured arm
- Used for proximal humerus fracture

### B. Transthoracic lateral (Lawrence method)
- Patient is upright with affected side against cassette
- Unaffected arm is raised above head
- Central ray passes through axilla to shoulder joint
- Very useful when shoulder cannot be abducted or rotated due to trauma

### C. Neer method (supraspinatus outlet view)
- Tangential projection
- Shows supraspinatus outlet and acromion process
- Useful in shoulder impingement syndrome

### D. Garth method (apical oblique)
- Special oblique view
- Best for posterior dislocation and shoulder instability

## 5. Clavicle

### Routine and special projections

| View | Purpose |
|---|---|
| AP clavicle | Shows clavicle fractures and alignment |
| AP axial clavicle | Better shows clavicle in a long projection |

### A. AP clavicle
- Patient is erect or supine
- Central ray perpendicular to clavicle
- Best for clavicle fracture and displacement

### B. AP axial clavicle
- Beam angled cephalad or caudal depending on position
- Gives clearer visualization of clavicle away from ribs
- Common in fracture evaluation

---

## 6. Acromioclavicular joint

### AP bilateral AC joints with and without weight
- Both joints are imaged together
- One film with weight-bearing and one without weight
- Purpose: evaluate AC joint separation

### Important exam point
- Weight-bearing view is more useful for joint stress and separation
- This is a common question in exams

---

## 7. Sternoclavicular joints

### Projection: PA, RAO, LAO
- Used to see sternoclavicular joint alignment and dislocation
- RAO/LAO are oblique views to avoid superimposition of ribs and spine

---

## 8. Scapula

| View | Purpose |
|---|---|
| AP scapula | Basic view of scapula |
| Scapular Y view | Used in shoulder dislocation |
| Lateral recumbent scapula | Demonstrates scapular body and spine |

### A. AP scapula
- Patient supine or erect
- Arm is abducted to 90°
- Shows scapula and surrounding structures

### B. Scapular Y view
- Patient in oblique position
- Useful to diagnose anterior or posterior dislocation
- The scapular body and glenoid appear as a Y shape

### C. Lateral scapula
- Patient in lateral recumbent position
- Best to view scapular body and spine

---

## 9. Most repeated exam points

### 1. Different between AP external and internal rotation
- External rotation: greater tubercle seen clearly
- Internal rotation: lesser tubercle and humeral head seen better

### 2. Why are special views used?
- To see structures not clearly visible in routine views
- To confirm dislocation, fracture, impingement or glenoid pathology

### 3. Why is Y view important?
- It helps differentiate anterior and posterior shoulder dislocation

### 4. Why is scapular outlet view used?
- To evaluate supraspinatus outlet and acromion impingement

### 5. Why AP AC joint with weight-bearing is done?
- To detect AC joint separation more accurately

---

## 10. Easy 5-mark answer pattern

### Example: Explain the positioning for shoulder joint

- Patient is placed in AP position
- Arm is rotated externally or internally depending on the view
- Humerus is centered to the shoulder joint
- Central ray is directed perpendicular to the shoulder joint
- Film is placed in contact with the shoulder region
- The view shows the humeral head, greater tubercle, lesser tubercle and glenoid cavity

This is a standard answer format for exams.

---

## 11. Short exam answer ready

“Shoulder girdle consists of clavicle, scapula and proximal humerus. Routine non-trauma projections include AP external rotation, AP internal rotation, axial and infero-superior axial views. Special views such as Grashley, West Point, Fisk and scapular Y view are used for glenoid cavity, bicipital groove and dislocation. Trauma views include AP neutral rotation, transthoracic lateral and Neer method. Clavicle is examined in AP and AP axial projections, while AC joints are evaluated with and without weight-bearing. Scapula is assessed in AP, Y view and lateral recumbent views.”
