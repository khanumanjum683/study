# Neck Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Neck radiography is an important chapter in radiographic positioning. The most common topics are:
- Related radiographic anatomy
- AP neck view
- Lateral neck view
- Soft tissue neck view
- Airway and neck trauma positioning

This topic is usually asked in short essay and short answer form.
The examiner wants:
- Patient position
- Central ray direction
- Structures shown
- Purpose of the view

---

## 2. Related radiographic anatomy
The neck region includes:
- Cervical vertebrae (C1 to C7)
- Soft tissues of the neck
- Pharynx
- Larynx
- Trachea
- Thyroid cartilage
- Hyoid bone
- Airway structures
- Carotid vessels (important but not seen clearly in X-ray)

### Important anatomy to remember
- Laryngeal cartilage
- Airway shadow
- Soft tissues of neck
- Cervical spine

## 3. AP neck view

### Indication
- Neck trauma
- Foreign body in neck
- Soft tissue swelling
- Airway problem
- Pre-operative check

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or erect
- AP position of neck
- The neck is centered to the cassette
- The chin is slightly raised to avoid jaw overlap

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the film
- Centered at the level of the larynx or cervical region

### Structures shown
- Cervical vertebrae
- Airway shadow
- Soft tissue of neck
- Laryngeal region

### Key points
- AP neck is used to study the soft tissues of the neck and cervical region
- Helps to identify enlargement of soft tissues or swelling

### Simple diagram
```text
     X-ray tube
         |
         V
   Patient in AP position
      Neck centered to film
       CR at laryngeal region
   Cervical spine and soft tissues seen
```

### Exam answer
"AP neck view is taken to visualize the cervical spine and soft tissues of the neck. The patient is placed in AP position with the neck centered to the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the film at the level of the larynx or upper cervical region. This view helps in detecting soft tissue swelling, structural abnormalities and cervical alignment."

---

## 4. Lateral neck view

### Indication
- Neck swelling
- Foreign body
- Soft tissue neck infection
- Trauma
- Airway obstruction

### Patient position
- Patient is in true lateral position
- Side of neck against cassette
- Neck is relaxed
- Shoulders are depressed if required

### Central ray
- Directed horizontal to the neck
- Centered to the level of the larynx or C4 region

### Structures shown
- Cervical vertebrae
- Soft tissues of neck
- Airway column
- Laryngeal and pharyngeal shadows

### Key points
- Lateral neck is very important for soft tissue neck study
- Best view to see air column, swelling or foreign body

### Diagram
```text
      X-ray tube
          |
          V
    Patient in true lateral position
        Neck against cassette
   Lateral view shows airway and soft tissue
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral neck view is the best projection for studying the soft tissues of the neck. The patient is placed in true lateral position with the side of the neck against the cassette. The central ray is directed horizontally through the laryngeal or C4 region. This view is useful for evaluating airway shadow, foreign bodies, swelling and cervical alignment."

---

## 5. Soft tissue neck view

### Indication
- Retropharyngeal abscess
- Epiglottitis
- Airway obstruction
- Soft tissue swelling
- Foreign body

### Patient position
- Patient is erect or supine
- Lateral projection is usually used
- The neck is placed in neutral position

### Central ray
- Directed horizontal to the neck
- Centered at the level of the larynx

### Structures shown
- Pharynx
- Larynx
- Trachea
- Soft tissue outline of the neck

### Key points
- This view is specifically used to study the soft tissues and airway
- It helps to identify swelling and obstruction

### Exam answer
"Soft tissue neck view is taken to assess the airway and soft tissues of the neck. The patient is positioned in lateral projection with the neck centered to the cassette. The central ray is directed horizontally to the laryngeal region. This view is useful for detecting swelling, foreign body, epiglottitis and airway obstruction."

## 6. Comparison table

| View | Main use | Important point |
|---|---|---|
| AP neck | Cervical spine and soft tissue survey | Chin slightly raised |
| Lateral neck | Soft tissue and airway study | True lateral |

## 8. Easy 5-mark answer
"Neck radiography includes AP neck view, lateral neck view and soft tissue neck view. AP neck view is taken to study the cervical spine and neck soft tissues. Lateral neck view is especially important because it shows the airway and soft tissues of the neck clearly. In lateral position, the patient is placed with the side of the neck against the cassette and the central ray is directed horizontally to the laryngeal region. Soft tissue neck view is used to identify foreign bodies, swelling and airway obstruction. These views are very useful in trauma and inflammatory conditions of the neck."

---

## 9. Final revision summary
Neck radiography is mainly done to study the cervical spine and soft tissues of the neck. The lateral neck view is the most important because it shows the airway and any soft tissue swelling clearly. AP neck is used for general evaluation and soft tissue neck view is used for airway obstruction and foreign body. Always mention patient position, central ray and structures shown in the exam.
