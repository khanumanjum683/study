# Foot Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Foot radiography is an important topic in lower limb radiographic positioning. The main views are:
- AP foot
- Oblique foot
- Lateral foot
- AP and lateral weight-bearing foot

For exam writing, always mention:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Central ray (CR)
- Structures shown
- Short exam answer

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of foot
The foot has many small bones. The main bones are:
- Tarsal bones
- Metatarsals
- Phalanges
- Calcaneus
- Talus
- Cuboid
- Navicular
- Cuneiform bones

Main joints:
- Tarsometatarsal joints
- Metatarsophalangeal joints
- Interphalangeal joints

Common structures seen in radiographs:
- Forefoot
- Midfoot
- Hindfoot
- Arch of the foot
- Sesamoid bones

## 3. AP foot

### Indication
- Foot pain
- Fracture of metatarsals or phalanges
- Foreign body
- Infection or bone lesion
- deformity

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or seated
- Foot is placed flat on the cassette
- Knee is flexed and leg is extended
- Sole is in contact with the film
- Toes are pointing upward

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the foot
- Centered to the base of the third metatarsal

### Structures shown
- Tarsal bones
- Metatarsals
- Phalanges
- Intertarsal joints
- Forefoot and midfoot

### Key points
- AP view is routine view for foot
- It shows the entire foot including toes and metatarsals
- Helpful in fracture and deformity evaluation

### Simple diagram
```text
      X-ray tube
          |
          V
    Foot flat on cassette
      Toes upward
      Tarsals   Metatarsals   Phalanges
                 AP Foot view
```

### Exam answer
"AP foot view is the routine projection for evaluating the foot. The patient is placed with the foot flat on the cassette and the toes pointing upward. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the base of the third metatarsal. This view shows the tarsal bones, metatarsals, phalanges and the joints of the foot clearly."

---

## 4. Oblique foot

### Indication
- Fracture
- Metatarsal injury
- Tarsal bone pathology
- Better view of certain bones

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or seated
- Foot is rotated medially or laterally by 30° to 45°
- The foot is placed on the cassette in oblique position

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the foot
- Centered to the midfoot

### Structures shown
- Cuboid
- Navicular
- Cuneiform bones
- Metatarsals
- Tarsal joints

### Key points
- Oblique foot view is used to see the tarsal bones more clearly
- It helps to identify fractures and dislocations
- Useful to evaluate the midfoot and lateral foot bones

### Simple diagram
```text
      Foot rotated 30°-45°
        Cuboid   Navicular
        Cuneiforms
       Oblique Foot view
```

### Exam answer
"Oblique foot view is taken by rotating the foot medially or laterally. This helps to visualize the tarsal bones and joints more clearly. The central ray is directed to the midfoot. This projection is useful for evaluating metatarsal fractures, cuboid and navicular involvement and midfoot pathology."

---

## 5. Lateral foot

### Indication
- Fracture of metatarsals or tarsals
- Pain and swelling
- Dislocation
- Bone lesion

### Patient position
- Patient lies on the affected side
- Foot is kept in true lateral position
- Heel and toes are aligned in the same plane
- The foot is extended and dorsiflexed slightly

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the foot
- Centered to the base of the metatarsals

### Structures shown
- Tarsals
- Metatarsals
- Calcaneus
- Talus
- Soft tissue structures

### Key points
- Lateral foot view is useful for checking alignment and fracture position
- It gives a better view of the longitudinal arch
- It is helpful in soft tissue evaluation

### Simple diagram
```text
    Patient on affected side
        Foot in true lateral
       Calcaneus   Talus
      Metatarsals
        Lateral Foot view
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral foot view is used to assess foot fractures and alignment. The patient is placed on the affected side with the foot in true lateral position. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the base of the metatarsals. This view shows the tarsal bones, calcaneus, talus and metatarsals in lateral relationship, helping in the evaluation of fracture and soft tissue abnormality."

---

## 6. AP and lateral weight-bearing foot

### Indication
- Flat foot
- Pes planus
- Longitudinal arch evaluation
- Foot deformity
- Weight-bearing stress abnormality

### Patient position
- Patient stands on the cassette
- Weight is equally distributed on both feet
- For AP weight-bearing: feet are flat and facing forward
- For lateral weight-bearing: one foot is placed in lateral position while weight is on it

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the midfoot or metatarsal region

### Structures shown
- Foot arches
- Metatarsals
- Tarsal bones
- Weight-bearing alignment of the foot

### Key points
- Weight-bearing views are important for evaluating foot arch and flat foot
- They show how the foot behaves under body weight
- Useful in flat foot and pain during walking

### Simple diagram
```text
      Weight-bearing AP foot
       body weight on both feet
            Arch line
       AP weight-bearing view

      Weight-bearing lateral foot
         One foot lateral
          Arch seen clearly
       Lateral weight-bearing view
```

### Exam answer
"AP and lateral weight-bearing foot views are taken to evaluate the longitudinal arch and foot alignment under body weight. The patient stands on the cassette with equal weight on both feet in AP view, while in lateral view one foot is placed in lateral position. The central ray is directed to the midfoot. These views are very useful for diagnosing flat foot, arch deformity and weight-bearing abnormalities."

## 7. Comparison table: Foot views

| View | Patient position | Central ray | Main use |
|---|---|---|---|
| AP foot | Foot flat on cassette | Midfoot / base of 3rd metatarsal | Routine foot evaluation |
| Oblique foot | Foot rotated 30°-45° | Midfoot | Better view of midfoot bones |
| Lateral foot | Affected side down, true lateral | Base of metatarsals | Fracture, alignment, arch |
| Weight-bearing foot | Standing on cassette | Midfoot | Flat foot, arch, weight-bearing deformity |

## 9. Easy 5-mark answer
"Foot radiography is usually done in AP, oblique and lateral views. In AP foot, the patient is positioned with the foot flat on the cassette and the toes pointing upward. The central ray is directed to the base of the third metatarsal. This view shows the tarsal bones, metatarsals and phalanges clearly. Oblique view is obtained by rotating the foot 30° to 45°, which helps to view the cuboid, navicular and cuneiform bones more clearly. Lateral foot view is taken with the patient on the affected side to assess fracture, alignment and arch. Weight-bearing foot views are taken to study the longitudinal arch and foot deformity under body weight."

---

## 10. One-line memory trick
- AP foot = flat foot, toes up
- Oblique foot = foot rotated
- Lateral foot = side lying
- Weight-bearing foot = standing foot, check arch

---

## 11. Final revision summary
Foot radiography is a common lower limb topic. AP view is the routine projection, oblique view shows the tarsal bones clearly, lateral view helps in fracture and alignment assessment, and weight-bearing views are important for flat foot and arch evaluation. Always remember the patient position, central ray and the structures shown. If you write these points clearly, you can score well in the exam.
