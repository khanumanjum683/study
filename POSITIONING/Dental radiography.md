## 2. Introduction
Dental radiography means taking X-rays of teeth and related structures. It helps in:
- detecting caries
- checking tooth infection
- evaluating jaw and bone diseases
- studying periodontal disease
- planning treatment

Dental radiographs are important in both diagnosis and treatment planning.

---

## 3. Important terminology

| Term | Meaning | Easy meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Intraoral | Film is inside the mouth | X-ray film placed in mouth |
| Extraoral | Film is outside the mouth | Film placed outside the mouth |
| Periapical | Shows whole tooth and surrounding bone | Root + tooth + bone around root |
| Bite wing | Shows crown and contact areas of upper and lower teeth | Best for caries between teeth |
| Occlusal | Large film placed on occlusal surface | Shows arch and floor of mouth |
| Cephalometry | Lateral skull head film | Used to study jaw, skull and growth |
| Orthopantomography | Panoramic x-ray of whole jaw | Whole upper and lower jaws in one film |

---

## 4. Dental formula
The dental formula is the arrangement of teeth in the mouth.

### Adult permanent dentition
- 2 incisors
- 1 canine
- 2 premolars
- 3 molars

In each half of upper and lower jaw:
- I = 2
- C = 1
- P = 2
- M = 3

So adult formula = 2,1,2,3

### Diagram
```text
Upper jaw:    2 1 2 3
Lower jaw:    2 1 2 3

This means each half jaw has 8 teeth.
```

### Important exam point
Dental formula helps to identify the type and number of teeth present.

## 5. Intraoral radiography
This means the X-ray film is placed inside the mouth.

### Common intraoral views
- Periapical view
- Bite wing view
- Occlusal view

### A. Periapical radiography
#### Indication
- Tooth pain
- Root infection
- Abscess
- Fracture of tooth
- Periodontal disease

#### Position
- Film is placed inside the mouth close to the tooth
- The tooth is in contact with the film

#### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the tooth and film

#### Structures shown
- Crown of tooth
- Root of tooth
- Periodontal ligament space
- Surrounding bone

#### Key points
- Best for root and adjacent bone
- Very useful in endodontic and periodontal problems

#### Exam answer
"Periapical radiography is an intraoral radiographic technique in which the film is placed inside the mouth close to the tooth. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the tooth and film. This view shows the whole tooth including the crown and root and also the surrounding bone. It is used in detecting caries, abscess, periodontal infection and tooth fractures."

---

### B. Bite wing radiography
#### Indication
- Interproximal caries
- Early decay between teeth
- Checking restoration margins

#### Position
- Patient bites on a small film holder
- The upper and lower teeth are in contact

#### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the film
- Through the crowns of the teeth

#### Structures shown
- Crowns of upper and lower teeth
- Interproximal surfaces
- Contact areas between teeth

#### Key points
- Best for interproximal caries
- Very common exam question

#### Diagram
```text
   Upper teeth       Contact area       Lower teeth
      |-------------------|------------------|
      crowns seen in bite wing view
```

#### Exam answer
"Bite wing radiography is an intraoral view used to detect caries between adjacent teeth. The patient bites on a film holder so that the crowns of upper and lower teeth are seen in contact. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the film. It demonstrates the crowns and interproximal surfaces clearly, which helps in detecting early carious lesions and restoration defects."

---

### C. Occlusal radiography
#### Indication
- Cyst
- Fracture of jaw
- Foreign body
- Large lesion in maxilla or mandible
- Salivary gland stones

#### Position
- Large film is placed on the occlusal surface of the upper or lower jaw

#### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the film

#### Structures shown
- Palate
- Floor of mouth
- Maxillary or mandibular arch
- Cysts and pathologies

#### Key points
- Useful for large lesions in the mouth
- Shows the arch and surrounding structures

#### Exam answer
"Occlusal radiography is an intraoral technique in which a large film is placed on the occlusal surface of the upper or lower arch. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the film. This view is used to detect fractures, cysts, foreign bodies and large lesions in the maxilla or mandible."

---

## 6. Extraoral radiography
This is done when the film is placed outside the mouth.

### A. Extraoral oblique lateral view
#### Indication
- TM joint assessment
- Mandibular fractures
- Deep lesions
- Facial trauma

#### Position
- Patient is in side position
- The head is rotated obliquely

#### Central ray
- Directed obliquely to the side of interest

#### Structures shown
- Mandibular body and ramus
- Temporomandibular region
- Deep cheek and jaw region

#### Key points
- This view helps to see the mandible beyond the normal intraoral area

---

## 7. Cephalometry
#### Indication
- Orthodontic treatment planning
- Jaw growth study
- Skeletal malocclusion

#### Position
- Lateral skull view with standard head position

#### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the film

#### Structures shown
- Skull base
- Jaw relation
- Facial profile

#### Key points
- Used mainly for orthodontics and facial growth analysis

#### Exam answer
"Cephalometry is an extraoral radiographic technique used for orthodontic and facial growth studies. The patient is positioned in a standard lateral skull position and the central ray is directed perpendicular to the film. It helps in studying the skeletal relationship of the jaws and facial structures."

---

## 8. Orthopantomography (OPG)
#### Indication
- Whole mouth survey
- Dental caries
- Periodontal disease
- Impacted teeth
- Jaw pathology

#### Position
- Patient stands or sits with the head in a special position
- The machine moves around the jaws

#### Central ray
- Rotates around the patient while the film or detector moves

#### Structures shown
- Upper and lower teeth
- Maxilla and mandible
- Jaw joints
- Condyles
- Impacted teeth

#### Key points
- OPG shows the whole mouth in one film
- It is one panoramic view of the maxilla and mandible
- Very useful for general dental evaluation

#### Exam answer
"Orthopantomography is a panoramic radiographic examination that gives a single image of the whole upper and lower jaw. The machine rotates around the patient while the film or detector moves. It is used to assess the teeth, impacted teeth, jaw fractures, cysts and other maxillofacial conditions."

## 9. Comparison table

| View | Best for | Main point |
|---|---|---|
| Periapical | Root and surrounding bone | Whole tooth with root |
| Bite wing | Interproximal caries | Crowns and contact areas |
| Occlusal | Large lesions / arch study | Big film on occlusal surface |
| Extraoral oblique lateral | Mandible and TM joint | Used outside mouth |
| Cephalometry | Orthodontic study | Skull and facial profile |
| OPG | Whole mouth survey | Panoramic view of jaw |

## 10. Very short exam notes
- Dental formula = 2,1,2,3
- Periapical = root + tooth + surrounding bone
- Bite wing = interproximal caries
- Occlusal = large lesion / arch study
- Cephalometry = orthodontic analysis
- OPG = panoramic whole jaw view

---

## 11. Easy 5-mark answer
"Dental radiography includes intraoral and extraoral techniques. Intraoral radiography includes periapical, bite wing and occlusal views. Periapical radiography is used to see the entire tooth and surrounding bone, while bite wing view is used mainly for interproximal caries. Occlusal view is used for large lesions and arch evaluation. Extraoral techniques include cephalometry and orthopantomography. Cephalometry is used in orthodontic treatment planning, while OPG gives a panoramic view of the whole jaw and is useful in diagnosing dental and jaw pathologies."

---

## 12. Final revision summary
Dental radiography is mainly used to study teeth, supporting bone and jaw structures. Intraoral radiographs are the most common and include periapical, bite wing and occlusal views. Extraoral radiographs such as cephalometry and OPG are used for broad jaw and facial assessment. Always remember the type of view, the area shown, and the main indication for the image.
