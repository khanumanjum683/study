# Calcaneum (Heel Bone) Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Calcaneum is the heel bone. It is a very common topic in lower limb radiography. The main views are:
- Lateral calcaneum
- Plantodorsal (axial) calcaneum

In exam answers, always mention:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Central ray (CR)
- Structures shown
- Why view is taken

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of calcaneum
The calcaneum is the largest tarsal bone.

Important parts:
- Calcaneal tuberosity
- Sustentaculum tali
- Trochlear process
- Medial and lateral surfaces
- Posterior articular facet
- Anterior and middle facets

It articulates with:
- Talus superiorly
- Cuboid anteriorly

Important relations:
- It forms the heel
- It helps in weight-bearing and walking
- It is important in fractures, heel pain and trauma

## 3. Lateral calcaneum view

### Indication
- Heel pain
- Fracture of calcaneum
- Trauma to heel
- Cancellous bone pathology
- Inflammation or osteomyelitis

### Patient position
- Patient lies on the affected side
- Foot is in true lateral position
- Heel is touching the cassette
- Leg is extended or slightly flexed depending on comfort
- The calcaneum is placed in contact with the film

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the calcaneum
- Centered to the calcaneal tuberosity or 1 inch below the medial malleolus

### Structures shown
- Calcaneum
- Talus
- Tarsal bones
- Heel region
- Fracture line if present

### Key points
- This is the routine projection for heel bone
- It shows the calcaneum clearly in lateral position
- Best view to see fracture, displacement and soft tissue swelling

### Simple diagram
```text
       X-ray tube
           |
           V
    Patient on affected side
      Heel in contact with cassette
      Calcaneum   Talus
       Lateral calcaneum view
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral calcaneum view is the routine projection for evaluating heel injuries. The patient is placed on the affected side with the heel in contact with the cassette and the foot in true lateral position. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the calcaneum, usually to the level of the heel or 1 inch below the medial malleolus. This view shows the calcaneum, talus and surrounding tarsal bones clearly and helps to detect fracture and displacement."

## 4. Plantodorsal (axial) calcaneum view

### Indication
- Heel fracture
- Calcaneal trauma
- Tarsal bone pathology
- Assessment of calcaneal tuberosity
- Evaluation of subtalar joint

### Patient position
- Patient is supine with leg extended
- Heel is placed on the cassette
- Foot is fully dorsiflexed
- The long axis of the foot is aligned with the central ray
- The beam is directed from below the foot upward

### Central ray
- Directed cephalad or towards the calcaneus
- Usually at 40° to the plane of the foot
- Centered to the base of the third metatarsal or calcaneal region

### Structures shown
- Calcaneum
- Sustentaculum tali
- Calcaneal tuberosity
- Subtalar joint
- Posterior articular facet

### Key points
- This view is taken to show the entire calcaneum, especially the tuberosity and subtalar region
- It is useful for heel fractures and calcaneal deformity
- It gives a clear view of the posterior aspect of the calcaneum

### Simple diagram
```text
      X-ray tube angled cephalad
              |
              V
      Foot dorsiflexed
       Heel on cassette
      Calcaneum seen in axial view
      Plantodorsal calcaneum view
```

### Exam answer
"Plantodorsal calcaneum view is taken to demonstrate the heel bone in axial projection. The patient is supine with the foot dorsiflexed and the heel placed on the cassette. The central ray is directed cephalad at 40° to the plane of the foot, passing through the calcaneal region. This view shows the calcaneum, sustentaculum tali, calcaneal tuberosity and subtalar region clearly and is useful for detecting heel fractures and deformities."

## 5. Comparison table: calcaneum views

|---|---|---|---|

## 7. Easy 5-mark answer
"Calcaneum is the heel bone. The two important views are lateral and plantodorsal axial. In lateral calcaneum view, the patient is placed on the affected side with the heel touching the cassette and the foot in true lateral position. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the calcaneum. This view helps in identifying fractures, displacement and soft tissue swelling. In plantodorsal view, the patient is supine with the foot dorsiflexed and the heel on the cassette. The central ray is directed cephalad at 40° to the plane of the foot. ## 8. One-line memory trick
- Lateral calcaneum = side lying, heel on film
- Axial calcaneum = foot dorsiflexed, beam angled cephalad

---

## 9. Final revision summary
Calcaneum is the heel bone and is commonly assessed by lateral and axial projections. Lateral view is used for routine fracture evaluation, while plantodorsal view gives a better look at the heel bone and subtalar area. Remember the patient position, central ray and structures shown because these are the points usually asked in exams.
