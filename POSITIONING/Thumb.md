# Thumb Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Thumb radiography is a very important topic in hand radiography. The common views are:
- AP thumb
- PA oblique thumb
- Lateral thumb
- AP (Robert's method)
- Skier's thumb (folio method)

The exam usually asks:
- Related radiological anatomy
- Positioning
- Indications
- Central ray direction
- Structures shown

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of thumb
The thumb is the first digit of the hand. It contains:
- Distal phalanx
- Proximal phalanx
- 1st metacarpal
- Metacarpophalangeal joint
- Interphalangeal joint
- Sesamoid bones near the thumb joint

The thumb is very important because it is commonly injured in trauma. It is also important in grip and function.

---

## 3. AP Thumb View

### Indication
- Fracture of thumb
- Dislocation of thumb
- Infection
- Arthritis
- Foreign body

### Position of patient
- Patient sits with the hand placed on the cassette
- The thumb is extended and placed in AP position
- Palm is down or the hand is slightly rotated depending on technique
- The thumb is not superimposed by other fingers

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the thumb joint
- Centered to the first metacarpophalangeal joint or interphalangeal joint

### Structures shown
- Distal phalanx
- Proximal phalanx
- 1st metacarpal
- MCP joint
- IP joint

### Key points
- This is the routine thumb view
- It helps in seeing the full thumb clearly
- It is used in fracture and dislocation evaluation

### Diagram
```text
X-ray tube
    |
    V
Thumb placed in AP position
    Distal phalanx
    Proximal phalanx
    1st metacarpal
```

### Exam answer
"AP thumb view is used for trauma and pathology of the thumb. The patient is positioned with the thumb placed in AP position and separated from the other fingers. The central ray is directed to the first metacarpophalangeal or interphalangeal joint. This view shows the distal phalanx, proximal phalanx and first metacarpal clearly."

---

## 4. PA Oblique Thumb View

### Indication
- Fracture of thumb
- Dislocation
- Joint pathology
- Better view of the metacarpal and phalanges

### Position of patient
- Hand is placed on the cassette
- Thumb is rotated 45° medially or laterally
- Fingers are separated to avoid overlapping

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the thumb
- Centered to the thumb base or MCP joint

### Structures shown
- Thumb bones in oblique position
- Better separation of the first metacarpal and phalanges
- Joints more clearly visible

### Key points
- This view is useful to reduce overlap of the thumb and adjacent finger structures
- It gives better detail of fracture line

### Diagram
```text
PA Oblique Thumb
Thumb rotated 45°
Less overlap
Better view of 1st metacarpal
```

### Exam answer
"PA oblique thumb view is used to reduce overlap of the thumb and adjacent structures. The thumb is rotated 45° and placed on the cassette. The central ray is directed to the first metacarpophalangeal joint. This view improves visualization of the thumb bones and joints and helps in identifying fractures."

---

## 5. Lateral Thumb View

### Indication
- Fracture displacement
- Thumb deformity
- Joint alignment
- Soft tissue swelling

### Position of patient
- Thumb is placed in true lateral position
- It is separated from other fingers
- Palm is placed on cassette, but the thumb is turned sideways

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the thumb
- Centered to the thumb joint

### Structures shown
- Distal phalanx
- Proximal phalanx
- 1st metacarpal
- IP and MCP joint
- Bone alignment

### Key points
- This view tells us if the thumb bones are aligned properly
- Very useful for fracture displacement and deformity

### Diagram
```text
Lateral Thumb View
Thumb side view
Bone alignment seen clearly
Useful for displacement and deformity
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral thumb view is used to assess alignment and displacement of the thumb bones. The thumb is placed in true lateral position, separated from the other fingers. The central ray is directed to the thumb joint. This view is especially useful in checking fracture displacement, deformity and joint alignment."

---

## 6. AP (Robert's method)

This is a special projection used mainly for the first metacarpal.

### Indication
- Fracture of first metacarpal
- Basilar thumb fracture
- CMC joint pathology
- First metacarpal displacement

### Position of patient
- Patient is positioned with the hand resting on the cassette
- The hand is pronated and the thumb is placed in a special angle position
- The first metacarpal is brought clear from the rest of the hand

### Central ray
- Directed to the first CMC joint
- Angled according to the standard technique

### Structures shown
- First metacarpal
- Trapezium
- CMC joint
- Carpal bones around the thumb

### Key points
- This view is very important in thumb base fractures
- It is also used to evaluate the first carpometacarpal joint

### Diagram
```text
AP (Robert's method)
Thumb base and 1st metacarpal shown clearly
CMC joint seen well
```

### Exam answer
"AP projection of thumb by Robert's method is used mainly for fracture of the first metacarpal and CMC joint. The hand is placed in a special position so that the thumb and first metacarpal are clear on the film. The central ray is directed to the first CMC joint. This view demonstrates the first metacarpal and trapezium clearly and is useful in injuries near the base of the thumb."

---

## 7. Skier's thumb (Folio method)

This is a very common special thumb projection.

### Indication
- Ulnar collateral ligament injury of thumb
- Skiers' thumb injury
- Thumb instability
- Ligamentous injury

### Position of patient
- Thumb is placed in a special stress position
- The hand is placed on cassette
- The thumb is slightly abducted or stressed depending on method

### Central ray
- Directed to the first MCP joint

### Structures shown
- Thumb MCP joint
- Ulnar collateral ligament region
- Any widening or tear in the ligament space

### Key points
- This is a stress view
- It is done to diagnose ligament injury when the normal thumb view appears normal
- It is very important in sports injuries

### Diagram
```text
Skier's thumb / folio method
Thumb stressed at MCP joint
Ligament space seen clearly
Widening indicates injury
```

### Exam answer
"Skier's thumb is a stress view of the thumb used to detect injury of the ulnar collateral ligament. The thumb is placed in a stress position at the first MCP joint and the central ray is directed to this region. The view shows widening of the ligament space if the ligament is torn. This method is important in sport-related thumb injuries."

---

## 8. Comparison table

| View | Main use | Special feature |
|---|---|---|
| AP thumb | Routine thumb evaluation | Shows thumb bones clearly |
| PA oblique thumb | Better detail | Reduces overlap |
| Lateral thumb | Alignment and displacement | Best for deformity check |
| AP Robert's method | First metacarpal and CMC joint | Base of thumb injury |
| Skier's thumb | Ligament injury | Stress view at MCP joint |

---

## 9. Very short exam notes
- AP thumb = routine view
- PA oblique = better separation
- Lateral = displacement and alignment
- Robert's method = base of thumb and first MC fracture
- Skier's thumb = ligament injury, stress view

---

## 10. Final easy 5-mark answer
"Thumb radiography is done in AP, oblique, lateral and special views. The AP thumb view is the routine projection and shows the distal phalanx, proximal phalanx and first metacarpal. The oblique view reduces overlap and helps in better visualization of the fracture line. The lateral view is used to assess displacement, alignment and deformity. Robert's method is used for first metacarpal and carpometacarpal joint injuries near the thumb base. Skier's thumb or folio method is a stress view used to detect ulnar collateral ligament injury of the thumb, especially in sports injuries."

---

## 11. Memory trick
- AP thumb = straight thumb
- Oblique = 45° rotation
- Lateral = side view
- Robert's method = base of thumb and CMC joint
- Skier's thumb = ligament injury, stress view

This topic is easy if you remember the purpose of each view and where the beam is directed.
