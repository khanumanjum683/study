# Leg Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Leg radiography is a common lower limb positioning topic. The main views are:
- AP leg
- Lateral leg

In exam answers, always mention:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Central ray (CR)
- Structures shown
- Why the view is taken

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of leg
The leg is formed by:
- Tibia (medial bone)
- Fibula (lateral bone)
- Knee joint above
- Ankle joint below

Important structures:
- Tibial tuberosity
- Tibial crest
- Medial malleolus
- Lateral malleolus
- Tibial shaft
- Fibular shaft
- Interosseous space

The leg is imaged mainly to assess:
- Fractures
- Bone deformity
- Infection
- Soft tissue swelling
- Tumour or pathology

---

## 3. AP Leg

### Indication
- Fracture of tibia and fibula
- Trauma to lower limb
- Infection or bone lesion
- Abnormality of leg bones

### Position of patient
- Patient is supine
- Leg is extended and placed flat on the cassette
- Knee is fully extended
- Ankle is included in the film
- Foot is dorsiflexed and slightly rotated internally

### Central ray
- Central ray is directed perpendicular to the leg
- Centered to the midshaft of the leg

### Structures shown
- Tibia
- Fibula
- Knee joint
- Ankle joint
- Interosseous space
- Soft tissues

### Key points
- AP view is the routine view for leg
- It shows both bones clearly
- Tibia and fibula should be seen in their correct relationship

### Simple diagram
```text
X-ray tube
    |
    V
Patient supine
Leg extended on cassette
Foot dorsiflexed
Knee joint --------------- Ankle joint
  Tibia      Fibula
         AP Leg view
```

### Exam answer format
"AP leg view is taken to evaluate fractures and bone pathology of the tibia and fibula. The patient is positioned supine with the leg extended and the foot slightly internally rotated. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the midshaft of the leg. This view shows the tibia, fibula, knee joint and ankle joint clearly."

---

## 4. Lateral Leg

### Indication
- Fracture displacement
- Bone alignment
- Soft tissue swelling
- Deformity of leg bones

### Position of patient
- Patient is lying on the affected side
- Leg is in true lateral position
- Knee and ankle are flexed slightly or placed straight in same plane
- Patella is facing forward
- Leg is aligned with the cassette

### Central ray
- Central ray is directed perpendicular to the leg
- Centered to the midshaft of the leg

### Structures shown
- Tibia and fibula in lateral relationship
- Knee joint and ankle joint
- Soft tissue shadows
- Fracture line or alignment problem

### Key points
- Lateral view is important to check displacement and alignment
- It helps to see fracture angulation and overlap
- Useful for assessing soft tissue swelling

### Simple diagram
```text
X-ray tube
    |
    V
Patient lying on affected side
Leg in true lateral position
Knee and ankle in same plane
Tibia and fibula overlap in lateral view
          Lateral Leg view
```

### Exam answer format
"Lateral leg view is used to assess fracture alignment and displacement of the tibia and fibula. The patient is positioned on the affected side with the leg in true lateral position and the knee and ankle kept in the same plane. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the midshaft of the leg. This view helps in identifying bone overlap, angulation and soft tissue swelling."

## 5. Comparison table: AP vs Lateral Leg

| View | Patient position | Central ray | Main use | Structures seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AP leg | Supine, leg extended, foot slightly internally rotated | Perpendicular to midshaft of leg | General bone study, fracture | Tibia, fibula, knee, ankle |
| Lateral leg | Affected side down, true lateral position | Perpendicular to midshaft of leg | Alignment, displacement, soft tissue | Tibia, fibula in lateral view, knee, ankle |

## 6. Very short exam notes

### AP leg
- Supine position
- Leg extended
- Foot slightly internally rotated
- Best for routine leg evaluation

### Lateral leg
- Affected side down
- True lateral position
- Knee and ankle in same plane
- Best for fracture alignment and displacement

---

## 7. Easy 5-mark answer
"Leg radiography is done mainly in AP and lateral projections. In AP leg, the patient is placed supine with the leg extended and the foot slightly internally rotated. The central ray is directed to the midshaft of the leg. This view shows the tibia, fibula, knee joint and ankle joint clearly. In lateral leg position, the patient lies on the affected side with the leg in true lateral position. The central ray is directed at the midshaft of the leg. This view is useful for checking fracture displacement, alignment, and soft tissue swelling."

---

## 8. One-line memory trick
- AP leg = leg straight, foot slightly turned in
- Lateral leg = side lying, true lateral position

---

## 9. Final revision summary
Leg radiography is an important lower limb topic. AP leg view is used for routine evaluation of tibia and fibula, while lateral leg view is used to assess alignment and fracture displacement. Always remember the patient position, central ray, and the joints included in the film. If you mention these points clearly in the exam, you can score well.
