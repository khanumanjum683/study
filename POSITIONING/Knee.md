# Knee Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Knee radiography is a very important topic in radiographic positioning. The main views are:
- Knee: AP
- Knee: oblique - medial and lateral rotations
- Knee: lateral
- Knee: skyline view
- AP (weight bearing)
- Knee: intercondylar fossa
- PA axial (camp Coventry and holmblad method)
- AP axial

This topic is often asked in short essay and short answer questions.

For every view, always mention:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Central ray (CR)
- Structures shown

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of knee
The knee joint is formed by:
- Distal femur
- Proximal tibia
- Patella
- Femoral condyles
- Tibial condyles
- Menisci
- Ligaments

Important bony structures:
- Medial condyle
- Lateral condyle
- Patella
- Tibial plateau
- Intercondylar fossa
- Fibular head

The knee is a weight-bearing joint, so weight-bearing views are very important in some conditions.

---

## 3. Knee: AP view

### Indication
- Knee pain
- Trauma
- Fracture
- Osteoarthritis
- Joint effusion

### Position of patient
- Patient stands or lies supine
- Knee is extended
- Leg is rotated slightly internally
- Patella is centered to the film

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the knee joint
- Centered to the joint space, below the patella

### Structures shown
- Distal femur
- Proximal tibia and fibula
- Patella
- Knee joint space
- Condyles

### Key points
- This is the routine view for knee joint
- It shows joint space and fractures
- Best for general knee evaluation

### Diagram
```text
Knee AP view
Patient supine or standing
Knee extended
  Distal femur
  Proximal tibia
  Patella
  Knee joint space
```

### Exam answer
"AP knee view is the routine projection used for knee trauma and pathology. The patient is positioned with the knee extended and the leg slightly internally rotated. The central ray is directed to the knee joint space. This view shows the distal femur, proximal tibia, patella and knee joint space clearly."

---

## 4. Knee: oblique view (medial and lateral rotation)

### Indication
- Fracture
- Meniscal injury
- Joint pathology
- Better view of tibial and femoral condyles

### Position of patient
- Patient is supine or standing
- Knee is rotated medially or laterally by 45°
- One side of the joint is brought into view more clearly

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the knee joint

### Structures shown
- Tibial condyles
- Femoral condyles
- Joint surfaces
- Meniscal region

### Key points
- Medial rotation and lateral rotation help in viewing different aspects of the knee
- Useful for assessing joint surfaces and tibial plateau

### Diagram
```text
Oblique knee
Knee rotated 45°
Medial or lateral rotation
Shows condyles and joint surface clearly
```

### Exam answer
"Oblique knee views are taken by rotating the knee medially or laterally. This helps in seeing the joint surfaces and condyles more clearly. The central ray is directed to the knee joint. These views assist in identification of fractures, meniscal pathology and joint surface changes."

---

## 5. Knee: lateral view

### Indication
- Fracture
- Knee effusion
- Patellar displacement
- Soft tissue swelling

### Position of patient
- Patient lies on the affected side
- Knee is flexed slightly
- Patella is placed perpendicular to the film
- Distal femur and proximal tibia are aligned

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the knee joint
- Centered to the joint space

### Structures shown
- Distal femur
- Proximal tibia
- Patella
- Knee joint space
- Soft tissue outline

### Key points
- Lateral view is useful for checking patellar position and displacement
- It gives good view of the joint space and soft tissues

### Diagram
```text
Lateral knee
Patient on side
Knee flexed slightly
  Distal femur
  Patella
  Proximal tibia
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral knee view is used to assess patellar position, fracture and soft tissue swelling. The patient is placed on the affected side with the knee slightly flexed. The central ray is directed to the knee joint. This view shows the patella, distal femur and proximal tibia clearly in lateral position."

---

## 6. Skyline view

### Indication
- Patellar fracture
- Patellar dislocation
- Patellar subluxation
- Patellar tracking problems

### Position of patient
- Patient is usually supine or standing
- Knee is flexed
- Patella is projected clearly by special angulation

### Central ray
- Directed tangential to the patella

### Structures shown
- Patella
- Patellar articular surface
- Femoral condyles

### Key points
- This view is used mainly for patella, not for general knee joint
- Shows patellar fracture and displacement clearly

### Diagram
```text
Skyline view
Knee flexed
Beam tangential to patella
Patella seen clearly
```

### Exam answer
"Skyline view is taken to assess the patella and its articular surface. The knee is flexed and the beam is directed tangentially to the patella. This view is helpful for patellar fracture, dislocation and subluxation."

---

## 7. AP weight bearing view

### Indication
- Joint space narrowing
- Osteoarthritis
- Meniscal tear
- Weight-bearing joint disease

### Position of patient
- Patient stands upright
- Both knees are in weight-bearing position
- Feet are placed in neutral position or slightly apart

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the knee joint
- Centered to the knee joint space

### Structures shown
- Distal femur
- Proximal tibia
- Joint space
- Meniscal region

### Key points
- This is a very important view because the knee is a weight-bearing joint
- It helps to assess joint space narrowing and osteoarthritis

### Diagram
```text
AP weight bearing knee
Patient standing
Both knees weight-bearing
  Knee joint space
  Distal femur
  Proximal tibia
```

### Exam answer
"AP weight-bearing knee view is used to assess the knee under body weight. The patient stands upright with both knees in weight-bearing position. The central ray is directed to the knee joint. This view is useful for detecting joint space narrowing, osteoarthritis and degenerative changes."

---

## 8. Knee: intercondylar fossa view

This is a special view for the knee joint.

### Indication
- Meniscal injury
- Osteochondral defect
- Loose bodies
- Joint pathology of intercondylar area

### Position of patient
- Patient is prone or supine depending on technique
- Knee is flexed to 40–50°
- Beam is angled as required

### Central ray
- Directed to the intercondylar fossa

### Structures shown
- Intercondylar fossa
- Femoral condyles
- Tibial plateau
- Posterior structures of knee

### Key points
- This view is helpful for intercondylar region pathology
- It demonstrates the knee joint from a different angle

### Diagram
```text
Intercondylar fossa view
Knee flexed 40–50°
Beam directed to intercondylar fossa
Shows fossa and posterior structures
```

### Exam answer
"Intercondylar fossa view is taken to better visualize the intercondylar region of the knee. The knee is flexed and the central ray is directed to the intercondylar fossa. This view shows the intercondylar notch, femoral condyles and posterior knee structures, helping in diagnosis of meniscal and osteochondral lesions."

---

## 9. PA axial (Camp Coventry and Holmblad method)

### Indication
- Intercondylar fossa pathology
- Osteochondral defects
- Meniscal injury

### Position of patient
- Patient is prone in Camp Coventry method
- Knee is flexed and the beam is angled
- In Holmblad method, patient kneels with knee flexed and beam directed perpendicular to the film

### Central ray
- Directed to the intercondylar fossa

### Structures shown
- Intercondylar fossa
- Posterior femoral condyles
- Tibial plateau

### Key points
- These are special views for intercondylar region
- Camp Coventry and Holmblad methods are both used for this purpose

### Diagram
```text
PA axial view
Knee flexed
Beam directed to intercondylar fossa
Shows fossa and posterior joint structures
```

### Exam answer
"PA axial view is used to assess the intercondylar fossa. In Camp Coventry method, the patient is prone with the knee flexed and the beam directed through the intercondylar region. In Holmblad method, the patient kneels with the knee flexed. Both methods are used to demonstrate the intercondylar fossa and related pathology."

---

## 10. AP axial

### Indication
- Patellar pathology
- Intercondylar region evaluation
- Some lower limb deformities

### Position of patient
- Patient may be supine or prone depending on technique
- Knee is flexed or angled as required

### Central ray
- Directed cephalad or caudad according to technique

### Structures shown
- Patella
- Femoral condyles
- Intercondylar region

### Key points
- AP axial is another special technique for knee evaluation
- Used when additional detail of patella and condyles is needed

---

## 11. Comparison table

| View | Main use | Special point |
|---|---|---|
| AP knee | Routine knee evaluation | Joint space and fractures |
| Oblique knee | Condylar and joint surfaces | Medial/lateral rotation |
| Lateral knee | Patella and displacement | Good for effusion and fracture |
| Skyline | Patellar fracture/dislocation | Tangential view |
| AP weight-bearing | OA, joint space narrowing | Weight-bearing evaluation |
| Intercondylar fossa | Posterior knee pathology | Fossa detail |
| PA axial | Intercondylar notch | Camp Coventry/Holmblad |
| AP axial | Special knee detail | Angled projection |

---

## 12. Very short exam notes
- AP = routine knee, joint space
- Oblique = condyles and joint surfaces
- Lateral = patella and displacement
- Skyline = patella fracture/dislocation
- Weight-bearing = osteoarthritis and joint space narrowing
- Intercondylar = fossa view
- PA axial = Camp Coventry / Holmblad

---

## 13. Final easy 5-mark answer
"Knee radiography includes AP, oblique, lateral, skyline, weight-bearing and special views. The AP knee view is the routine projection and shows the distal femur, proximal tibia, patella and knee joint space. Oblique views are taken with medial or lateral rotation to show the condyles and joint surfaces more clearly. The lateral view is useful in patellar displacement and soft tissue swelling. Skyline view is used for patellar fracture and dislocation. Weight-bearing AP view is important in osteoarthritis and joint space narrowing. Intercondylar fossa and PA axial views are special projections used to demonstrate the intercondylar region and posterior knee structures."

---

## 14. Memory trick
- AP knee = routine
- Oblique = rotated 45°
- Lateral = side view
- Skyline = patella view
- Weight-bearing = arthritis and joint space
- Intercondylar = fossa view

This topic is scoring when you remember the purpose and the position of each view.
