# Skull, Cranial and Facial Bones

Skull and facial bones are very important in radiographic positioning.
Most questions ask about:
- Related radiographic anatomy
- Basic and special projections
- Cranium

## 3. Basic skull views

### A. Lateral skull view

#### Indication
- Skull trauma
- Fracture
- Cranial deformity
- Sella turcica study

#### Patient position
- Patient is in true lateral position
- Side of head touching the cassette
- MSP parallel to cassette
- Interpupillary line perpendicular to cassette

#### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered 2.5 cm above the external auditory meatus

#### Structures shown
- Cranial bones
- Sutures
- Sella turcica
- Paranasal sinuses
- Temporal bone region

#### Key points
- It is very useful for skull anatomy
- Best for sella turcica and cranial vault

#### Exam answer
"Lateral skull view is taken to demonstrate the skull bones, cranial sutures and sella turcica. The patient is placed in true lateral position with the side of the head against the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the cassette at 2.5 cm above the external auditory meatus. This view shows the cranial vault, sella turcica and paranasal sinus region."

---

### B. PA skull view

#### Indication
- Frontal bone pathology
- Cranial deformity
- Skull trauma

#### Patient position
- Patient is prone or facing the cassette
- Forehead and nose touching the cassette

#### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered at the nasion

#### Structures shown
- Frontal bone
- Orbital rims
- Cranium
- Frontal sinus region

#### Key points
- Best view for frontal bone and cranium
- Commonly used for frontal projection

#### Exam answer
"PA skull view is taken to show the frontal bone and anterior cranial vault. The patient is positioned with the forehead and nose against the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the nasion. This projection mainly demonstrates the frontal bone, orbital rims and cranial vault."

---

### C. AP skull view

#### Indication
- Skull trauma
- Cranial pathology
- When PA view is not possible

#### Patient position
- Patient is supine or erect AP position

#### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered to the nasion or glabella

#### Structures shown
- Whole skull
- Frontal bone
- Occipital region

#### Key points
- AP skull is used when patient cannot be positioned prone

---

## 4. Special skull views

### A. Caldwell view

#### Indication
- Frontal sinus
- Orbit
- Facial bones

#### Patient position
- PA projection
- Patient faces the film
- The head is tilted slightly down

#### Central ray
- Directed 15° caudad
- Centered to the nasion

#### Structures shown
- Frontal sinus
- Frontal bone
- Orbital floor

#### Key points
- Caldwell view is best for frontal sinus
- Important exam question: “Explain the projection for frontal sinus”

#### Exam answer
"Caldwell view is a PA projection with 15° caudal angulation. The patient is positioned with the forehead and nose touching the cassette and the central ray directed 15° caudad to the nasion. This view is mainly used to demonstrate the frontal sinus and orbital floor."

---

### B. Waters view

#### Indication
- Maxillary sinus
- Facial bones
- Orbit

#### Patient position
- Patient is PA position
- Chin is raised so that the nose is 1–2 cm away from the film

#### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered to the acanthion

#### Structures shown
- Maxillary sinus
- Orbital floor
- Facial bones

#### Key points
- Best view for maxillary sinus
- Common short exam answer

#### Exam answer
"Waters view is used to show the maxillary sinus clearly. The patient is placed in PA position with the chin raised so that the nose is slightly away from the film. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the acanthion. This view is very useful for maxillary sinus and facial bone study."

---

### C. Townes view

#### Indication
- Occipital bone
- Foramen magnum
- Cranial base

#### Patient position
- AP projection
- Patient is supine or erect
- The chin is depressed

#### Central ray
- Directed 30° caudad
- Centered to the foramen magnum region

#### Structures shown
- Occipital bone
- Foramen magnum
- Base of skull

#### Key points
- Townes view is best for occipital bone

---

### D. Submentovertex view (SMV)

#### Indication
- Base of skull
- Sphenoid sinus
- Foramen ovale and cranial base

#### Patient position
- Patient is supine or erect
- Head is hyperextended
- Vertex of skull touches the cassette

#### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered to the midpoint between the angles of mandible

#### Structures shown
- Base of skull
- Mandibular condyles
- Foramina

#### Key points
- Very useful for base of skull

---

## 5. Sella turcica

### Indication
- Pituitary gland lesions
- Skull base pathology

### View used
- Lateral skull view is best

### Structures shown
- Sella turcica
- Pituitary fossa

### Key points
- The sella turcica is a saddle-shaped structure in the sphenoid bone

### Exam answer
"Sella turcica is a saddle-shaped depression in the sphenoid bone. It is best demonstrated on the lateral skull view. This view is used to assess pituitary fossa and sellar region."

---

## 6. Mastoids

### Indication
- Mastoid infection
- Mastoid air cells
- Temporal bone pathology

### Common views
- Lateral mastoid view
- Axial mastoid view

### Important points
- Mastoid air cells are seen in mastoid projection
- Helps in detecting infection or disease

## 7. Optic foramina and orbits

### View used
- Rhese view or optic foramen view

#### Patient position
- Patient is prone or facing the film
- Head is rotated 35° to 40°

#### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette

#### Structures shown
- Optic foramen
- Orbit
- Superior orbital fissure

#### Key points
- This is used to evaluate optic foramen and orbit

### Exam answer
"Optic foramen view is a special skull projection used to visualize the optic foramen and orbit. The patient is rotated 35°-40° with the orbit of the side under examination close to the film. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the cassette. This view is useful in studying the optic canal and orbital region."

## 9. TM joint (Temporomandibular joint)

### Indication
- TM joint pain
- Dislocation
- Arthritis

### Views used
- Open and closed mouth projection
- Lateral TM joint view

### Key points
- TM joint is studied in open and closed mouth positions
- It helps to see condyle movement and displacement

### Exam answer
"TM joint radiography is performed in open and closed mouth positions to study the temporomandibular joint. The patient is positioned according to the projection and the condylar movement is evaluated. This helps in diagnosing dislocation, arthritis or dysfunction."

## 13. Paranasal sinuses

### Important sinuses
- Frontal sinus
- Maxillary sinus
- Ethmoid sinus
- Sphenoid sinus

### Common views
- Waters view → maxillary sinus
- Caldwell view → frontal sinus
- Lateral sinus view → sphenoid and ethmoid sinus

### Exam answer
"Paranasal sinuses are studied mainly by Waters, Caldwell and lateral views. Waters view best demonstrates the maxillary sinus, Caldwell view shows the frontal sinus and lateral view helps in evaluating sphenoid and ethmoid sinuses."

## 15. Very short exam notes
- Skull = cranium + facial bones
- Lateral skull = sella turcica
- PA skull = frontal bone
- Caldwell = frontal sinus
- Waters = maxillary sinus
- Townes = occipital bone and foramen magnum
- SMV = base of skull
- Rhese = optic foramen
- TM joint = open and closed mouth
- Zygomatic arch = special view
- Mandible = body, ramus, angle, condyle

---

## 16. Easy 5-mark answer
"The skull is divided into cranial bones and facial bones. The most common skull projections are lateral skull, PA skull, Caldwell, Waters and Townes. Lateral skull is used to visualize the sella turcica and cranial vault. PA skull is used for the frontal bone. Caldwell view is best for the frontal sinus, while Waters view is best for the maxillary sinus. Special projections like SMV and Rhese view help in studying the base of skull and optic foramen respectively. These views are important for diagnosis of fractures, sinus disease and craniofacial pathology."

---

## 17. Final revision summary
Skull radiography includes basic and special views. Lateral skull shows the cranial vault and sella turcica. Caldwell and Waters are important for frontal and maxillary sinuses. Townes and SMV are used for occipital bone and skull base. Rhese is used for optic foramen and orbit. Always remember the patient position, central ray, and the structure shown. If you write these points clearly, you can score very well.
