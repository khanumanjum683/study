# Elbow Radiography: Easy Exam Notes

## 1. Related radiological anatomy

The elbow joint is made by:
- Humerus (upper arm bone)
- Radius (outer forearm bone)
- Ulna (inner forearm bone)

Important structures to identify on X-ray:
- Medial epicondyle
- Lateral epicondyle
- Trochlea
- Capitulum
- Olecranon
- Radial head
- Radial neck
- Coronoid process

## 2. Elbow radiographic views

| View | Position / technique | Main structure seen | Important point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Related radiological anatomy | Know elbow bones and landmarks | Humerus, radius, ulna, olecranon, radial head | Very important for anatomy-based questions |
| AP full extension | Elbow fully straight | General elbow joint | Standard elbow X-ray |
| AP partially flexed | Elbow slightly bent | Better for mild trauma | Used when full extension is painful |
| AP oblique external rotation | Arm rotated outward by 45° | Radial head and neck | Best view for radial head injury |
| AP oblique internal rotation | Arm rotated inward by 45° | Coronoid process and trochlea | Best for medial structures |
| Lateral view | Elbow flexed 90° | Olecranon in profile; joint details | Best for fracture and effusion |
| Acute flexion (Jones method) | Elbow sharply flexed | Olecranon and distal humerus | Used in severe trauma |
| Trauma axial lateral (Coyle method) | Special trauma projection | Radial head or coronoid process | Used in difficult cases |
| Radial head lateral | Focused lateral view | Radial head and neck | Useful for radial head fractures |

## 3. Explanation of each view in easy English

### A. Related radiological anatomy
This means the student must know the normal elbow structures seen on X-ray.

You should be able to identify:
- Distal humerus
- Olecranon
- Radial head
- Coronoid process
- Medial and lateral epicondyles

Exam point: If the question says “related radiological anatomy,” they usually expect identification of bones and landmarks.

---

### B. AP full extension
This is the standard elbow view.

How it is done:
- Elbow is fully extended (straight)
- Palm usually facing upward
- X-ray beam passes through elbow joint

What it shows:
- Humerus
- Radius
- Ulna
- Elbow joint
- Radial head

Why it is important:
- Basic projection for elbow fractures
- Helpful for checking joint alignment

Memory tip:
AP full extension = “straight elbow, basic view”

---

### C. AP partially flexed
Sometimes the patient cannot fully straighten the elbow because of pain or injury.

So the elbow is slightly bent instead of fully extended.

Why used:
- In trauma
- In painful conditions
- When full extension is difficult

What it shows:
- Similar structures as AP full extension
- Useful when straight position is not possible

Memory tip:
Partially flexed = “slightly bent elbow”

---

### D. AP oblique external rotation
The forearm is rotated outward by about 45°.

This is the best view to see the radial head.

What it shows:
- Radial head
- Radial neck
- Lateral structures
- Capitulum

Why important:
- Most useful for radial head fractures

Memory tip:
External rotation = “radial head view”

---

### E. AP oblique internal rotation
The forearm is rotated inward by about 45°.

What it shows:
- Coronoid process
- Trochlea
- Medial structures

Why important:
- Useful to assess medial elbow structures
- Helps detect coronoid process fractures

Memory tip:
Internal rotation = “coronoid / trochlea view”

---

### F. Lateral view
This is one of the most important elbow projections.

How it is done:
- Elbow flexed to 90°
- Arm placed in true lateral position
- Thumb up

What it shows:
- Olecranon in profile
- Radial head over coronoid process
- Condyles superimposed

Why important:
- Best view for elbow joint effusion
- Helpful in fracture assessment
- Very important in trauma

Memory tip:
Lateral view = “90° flexion, olecranon profile”

---

### G. Acute flexion (Jones method)
This is done when the elbow is fixed in a sharply bent position after injury.

Why used:
- Severe trauma
- When the elbow cannot be extended

What it shows:
- Relationship between olecranon and distal humerus

Why important:
- Helps when standard views are difficult

Memory tip:
Acute flexion = “strongly bent elbow in trauma”

---

### H. Trauma axial lateral (Coyle method)
This is a special trauma projection.

Why used:
- When patient cannot rotate the elbow normally
- When standard views are limited because of pain or fracture

What it shows:
- Radial head or coronoid process depending on beam angle

Why important:
- Useful in difficult trauma cases

Memory tip:
Coyle method = “special trauma view”

---

### I. Radial head lateral
This is a focused lateral view of the radial head.

What it shows:
- Radial head
- Radial neck

Why important:
- Very helpful in radial head fracture detection

Memory tip:
Radial head lateral = “radial head fracture view”

---

## 4. Quick comparison for exam

| View | Best seen structure | Exam significance |
|---|---|---|
| AP full extension | General elbow joint | Standard elbow view |
| AP oblique external rotation | Radial head | Radial head fracture |
| AP oblique internal rotation | Coronoid process / trochlea | Medial elbow structures |
| Lateral | Olecranon, joint details | Best for elbow trauma and effusion |
| Acute flexion | Distal humerus and olecranon | Severe trauma / limited movement |
| Coyle method | Radial head or coronoid in trauma | Special trauma projection |

---

## 5. Final exam summary

The elbow is a complex joint and different radiographic views are used to see different structures. The AP view gives the basic anatomy, while the lateral view is the most important for joint details and fracture assessment. Oblique external rotation clearly demonstrates the radial head, and oblique internal rotation shows the coronoid process and trochlea. In trauma, special views like Jones method and Coyle method are used when the patient cannot fully extend or rotate the elbow.

---

## 6. Very short memory lines for revision

- AP full extension = standard elbow view
- Lateral = best for elbow joint details
- External oblique = radial head
- Internal oblique = coronoid process
- Coyle = trauma view
- Jones = acute flexion view

---

## 7. 5-mark answer format

“Radiological anatomy of the elbow includes the distal humerus, olecranon, radial head, coronoid process, and epicondyles. The AP full extension view is the standard projection showing the elbow joint. The lateral view is taken at 90° flexion and best demonstrates the olecranon in profile. AP oblique external rotation reveals the radial head, while AP oblique internal rotation visualizes the coronoid process and trochlea. In trauma, special views like Jones method and Coyle method are used when elbow movement is restricted.”

---

## 8. Easy revision trick

Use this order:

1. AP full extension – general anatomy
2. Lateral – best joint view
3. External oblique – radial head
4. Internal oblique – coronoid process
5. Trauma views – Coyle / Jones

This is the easiest way to remember the elbow projections for your exam.
