# Thoracic Spine Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Thoracic spine is an important chapter in radiographic positioning. The main views are:
- AP thoracic spine
- Lateral thoracic spine
- Oblique thoracic spine

Thoracic spine is usually studied to see:
- Vertebral bodies
- Intervertebral disc spaces
- Spinous processes
- Transverse processes
- Rib articulation
- Fractures, deformities, or pathology

In the exam, always write:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Part position
- Central ray
- Structures shown

---

## 2. Related radiographic anatomy
The thoracic spine consists of 12 thoracic vertebrae.

Important anatomy:
- Thoracic vertebral bodies
- Intervertebral disc spaces
- Spinous processes
- Transverse processes
- Pedicles
- Facet joints
- Costovertebral joints
- Rib articulations

The thoracic spine is the mid part of the vertebral column.
It lies between the cervical spine and lumbar spine.

### Simple anatomy diagram
```text
       CERVICAL SPINE        THORACIC SPINE         LUMBAR SPINE
           |                       |                     |
           |-----------------------|---------------------|
           C1-C7                 T1-T12               L1-L5

Important thoracic landmarks:
T1 = upper thoracic region
T3-T6 = middle thoracic vertebrae
T12 = lower thoracic region
```

## 3. AP thoracic spine view

### Indication
- Thoracic pain
- Fracture
- Scoliosis
- Kyphosis
- Infection
- Tumor

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or erect
- The body is centered to the cassette
- The patient is in AP position
- Arms are placed by the side of the body

### Part position
- Spine should be straight and midline
- The chin is slightly raised to prevent jaw overlap
- Knees may be flexed in supine position to reduce lumbar lordosis

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the thoracic spine
- Centered to the level of T6 or T7
- Sometimes a 5° to 10° cephalad angle may be used depending on technique

### Structures shown
- Thoracic vertebral bodies
- Intervertebral spaces
- Spinous processes
- Transverse processes
- Costovertebral joints

### Key points
- AP view is used to see thoracic vertebrae clearly
- It shows vertebral bodies and disc spaces
- It is useful for general survey of thoracic spine

### Simple diagram
```text
      X-ray tube
          |
          V
    Patient in AP position
       Thoracic spine centered
     CR to T6-T7
   Vertebral bodies and disc spaces seen
```

### Exam answer
"AP thoracic spine view is taken to demonstrate the thoracic vertebral bodies, intervertebral spaces and spinous processes. The patient is placed in supine or erect position with the thoracic spine centered to the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the mid-thoracic region, usually at T6-T7. This view is useful in evaluating fractures, deformities and general thoracic spine pathology."

## 4. Lateral thoracic spine view

### Indication
- Fracture
- Kyphosis
- Scoliosis
- Disc disease
- Tumor
- Infection

### Patient position
- Patient is in true lateral position
- Side of thorax against the cassette
- Arms are raised above the head if possible
- The spine is parallel to the film

### Part position
- Patient’s shoulders are placed in line
- A pillow may be used between knees for comfort if supine
- The thoracic spine should be centered to the cassette

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the thoracic spine
- Centered to the level of T6 or T7
- A small caudal angle may be used if needed

### Structures shown
- Vertebral bodies
- Intervertebral disc spaces
- Spinous processes
- Thoracic vertebral alignment
- Kyphotic deformity or fracture line

### Key points
- Lateral view is the best view for vertebral bodies and disc spaces
- It is commonly used in trauma and deformity
- The ribs and shoulder may overlap, so arm elevation is helpful

### Simple diagram
```text
      X-ray tube
          |
          V
   Patient in true lateral position
      Arms raised above head
   Spine parallel to cassette
   Lateral thoracic spine view
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral thoracic spine view is taken to demonstrate the vertebral bodies, disc spaces and alignment of the thoracic spine. The patient is placed in true lateral position with the thoracic spine against the film. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the mid-thoracic spine at T6-T7. This view is very useful for detecting fractures, deformities and disc pathology."

---

## 5. Oblique thoracic spine view

### Indication
- Facet joint pathology
- Nerve root irritation
- Zygapophyseal joint study
- Small fractures

### Patient position
- Patient is in semilateral or oblique position
- Body is rotated 20° to 30°
- One side is closer to the film

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the thoracic spine
- Centered to T6-T7

### Structures shown
- Zygapophyseal joints
- Facet joints
- Thoracic vertebral bodies

### Key points
- Oblique view is mainly for facet joints
- It helps to study joints in thoracic region
- Used less commonly than AP and lateral views

### Simple diagram
```text
      Patient rotated 20°-30°
          /      \
         /  Oblique \
        /            \
       Film near one side
   Facet joints and zygapophyseal joints seen
```

### Exam answer
"Oblique thoracic spine view is taken to visualize the zygapophyseal joints and thoracic facet joints. The patient is rotated 20° to 30° from the lateral position with the side of interest near the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the thoracic spine at T6-T7. This view is useful in assessing joint pathology and small fractures."

## 6. Comparison table

| View | Main use | Important point |
|---|---|---|
| AP thoracic spine | General survey | Shows vertebral bodies and disc spaces |
| Lateral thoracic spine | Best for alignment and fracture | True lateral position |
| Oblique thoracic spine | Facet joint study | 20°–30° rotation |

## 7. Very short exam notes

### AP thoracic spine
- Patient: AP position
- CR: perpendicular to T6-T7
- Shows: vertebral bodies, disc spaces, spinous processes

### Lateral thoracic spine
- Patient: true lateral
- CR: perpendicular to T6-T7
- Shows: vertebral alignment, disc spaces, fractures

### Oblique thoracic spine
- Patient: 20°–30° oblique
- CR: perpendicular to T6-T7
- Shows: zygapophyseal/facet joints

---

## 8. Easy 5-mark answer
"Thoracic spine is examined by AP, lateral and oblique views. The AP thoracic spine view is taken with the patient in AP position and the central ray directed to T6-T7. It shows the vertebral bodies, intervertebral spaces and spinous processes. The lateral view is done in true lateral position and is the best view to study vertebral alignment, disc spaces and fractures. The oblique view is taken with 20° to 30° rotation to visualize the facet joints. These views are useful to assess thoracic vertebral pathology, deformities and trauma."

---

## 9. Final revision summary
Thoracic spine is mainly examined by AP, lateral and oblique views. The AP view shows vertebral bodies and disc spaces; lateral is the most important view for alignment and fractures; oblique view helps to see facet joints. In exam answers, always mention patient position, central ray and structures shown. This is the easiest way to score.
