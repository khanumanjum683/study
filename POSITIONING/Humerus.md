# Humerus: Easy Exam Notes

This chapter is very important in radiographic positioning. In many papers, the examiner asks:

- Humerus AP view
- Humerus lateral view
- Horizontal beam lateral humerus
- Proximal humerus views
- Related radiological anatomy
- Fracture and trauma positioning

This note is made in simple English so you can understand and write better answers in the exam.

---

## 1. Simple meaning of humerus

The humerus is the long bone of the upper arm.

It extends from:
- shoulder joint at the upper end
- elbow joint at the lower end

So, humerus is important for:
- shoulder movements
- elbow movements
- fracture assessment
- dislocation evaluation

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of humerus

### A. Upper end of humerus

| Structure | Meaning | Exam importance |
|---|---|---|
| Head of humerus | Rounded smooth upper end | Fits into glenoid cavity of scapula |
| Anatomical neck | Slightly narrowed area below head | Common landmark |
| Surgical neck | Narrowest part below tubercles | Very common fracture site |
| Greater tubercle | Large bony projection on lateral side | Seen clearly in external rotation |
| Lesser tubercle | Small projection on anterior side | Seen well in internal rotation |
| Intertubercular groove | Groove between tubercles | Contains long head of biceps tendon |
| Deltoid tuberosity | Rough area on shaft | Attachment of deltoid muscle |

### B. Shaft of humerus

- Long cylindrical middle part
- Provides strength and support
- Contains the shaft of the bone and muscle attachment areas

### C. Lower end of humerus

| Structure | Meaning | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| Medial epicondyle | Medial bony prominence | Important landmark for elbow |
| Lateral epicondyle | Lateral bony prominence | Important landmark for elbow |
| Trochlea | Pulley-like medial articular surface | Articulates with ulna |
| Capitulum | Rounded lateral articular surface | Articulates with radius |
| Olecranon fossa | Depression on posterior side | Accommodates olecranon during extension |
| Coronoid fossa | Depression on anterior side | Receives coronoid process |

### Easy memory trick

- Head = shoulder end
- Tubercle = projection / bump
- Surgical neck = common fracture site
- Epicondyles = elbow prominences
- Trochlea + capitulum = elbow joint surfaces

## 3. Humerus AP view

### Purpose
This view is used to examine the entire humerus, especially:
- shaft
- upper end
- lower end
- fractures
- dislocations
- bone pathology

### Position
- Patient is erect or supine
- Arm is extended and rotated externally if needed
- Humerus is placed in true AP position
- Elbow is slightly extended

### Part position
- Posterior aspect of the humerus touches the cassette
- The arm is rotated so the epicondyles are parallel to the film
- Shoulder and elbow should be included on the image

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the midshaft of the humerus
- Center at the shaft and include both shoulder and elbow joints

### Structures shown
- Humeral shaft
- Surgical neck
- Greater tubercle
- Lesser tubercle
- Distal humerus
- Elbow joint

### Important exam note
In AP humerus, the epicondyles are parallel to the cassette. This makes the humerus appear in true AP position.

---

## 4. Humerus lateral view

### Purpose
This view is used to see the humerus in side view and to study:
- shaft fracture
- alignment of bone
- elbow joint
- humeral head and tubercles

### Position
- Patient is in lateral position
- Affected arm is placed side by side with the cassette
- Elbow is flexed 90° if possible

### Part position
- Arm is rotated medially so the epicondyles are superimposed
- The body is rotated to obtain true lateral position
- Shoulder and elbow both included

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the mid-humeral shaft

### Structures shown
- Humeral shaft in lateral view
- Superimposition of epicondyles
- Lower end of humerus
- Elbow joint and olecranon process

### Important exam point
In lateral projection, the epicondyles are superimposed. This gives a side view of the humerus.

---

## 5. AP vs Lateral humerus: easy comparison

| Feature | AP humerus | Lateral humerus |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Patient facing X-ray beam | Side view of humerus |
| Arm rotation | Epicondyles parallel | Epicondyles superimposed |
| Main purpose | General humerus and shaft | Side view and alignment |
| Fracture view | Useful | Very useful for displacement |
| Tubercle visibility | Greater tubercle is visible | Better lateral relationship |

### Very short exam answer
- AP humerus shows the humerus in front-to-back view.
- Lateral humerus shows the humerus in side view.
- AP is used for general bone outline and fracture detection.
- Lateral is used to see exact position and displacement of fracture.

---

## 6. Horizontal beam lateral humerus (trauma view)

### Purpose
This is done in trauma when the patient cannot move the arm or shoulder normally.

### Position
- Patient is supine or erect
- Affected arm is kept in a natural position
- A horizontal beam is used instead of standard projection

### Why necessary?
- To avoid movement of injured arm
- To avoid severe pain and discomfort
- To study fracture line and alignment

### Important point
This is very important in shoulder and proximal humerus trauma.

### Structures shown
- Proximal humerus
- Humeral shaft
- Shoulder joint region
- Fracture in upper end of humerus

### Easy sentence for exam
“Horizontal beam lateral humerus is a trauma projection used when patient cannot rotate or abduct the arm; it helps in the assessment of proximal humerus fractures without causing further movement.”

## 7. Proximal humerus views

The proximal humerus is the upper end near the shoulder. This area is very important because fractures often occur here.

### Common proximal humerus views

| View | Purpose | Special use |
|---|---|---|
| AP external rotation | Shows greater tubercle clearly | Standard shoulder anatomy |
| AP internal rotation | Shows lesser tubercle and humeral head better | Joint and tubercle study |
| Transthoracic lateral | Shows proximal humerus when arm cannot move | Trauma case |
| Axillary projection | Shows shoulder joint and glenoid region | Dislocation evaluation |

### A. AP external rotation
- Arm is externally rotated
- Greater tubercle becomes clearly visible
- Used for shoulder anatomy and fracture assessment

### B. AP internal rotation
- Arm is internally rotated
- Lesser tubercle and humeral head are better shown
- Common in shoulder evaluation

### C. Transthoracic lateral
- Patient stands or sits with affected side against IR
- Unaffected arm is raised above head
- Beam passes through thorax to shoulder region
- Used when the patient cannot abduct or rotate the arm due to pain

### D. Axillary shoulder view
- Arm is abducted
- Useful to assess glenohumeral joint and dislocation

## 8. Common fracture sites in humerus

### A. Surgical neck fracture
- Most common fracture site of proximal humerus
- Usually occurs near surgical neck
- Often caused by fall on outstretched hand

### B. Shaft fracture
- Fracture in middle part of humerus
- May affect radial nerve

### C. Supracondylar fracture
- Occurs just above elbow joint
- Very common in children

### D. Condylar fracture
- Near lower end of humerus

### Exam memory sentence
“Proximal humerus fracture commonly occurs at the surgical neck; this is a very important radiographic landmark.”

## 10. Easy short answer format for exam

### 1. What is humerus?
The humerus is the long bone of the upper arm extending from shoulder to elbow.

### 2. What is the common fracture site of humerus?
The surgical neck of the humerus is the common fracture site.

### 3. What is the purpose of AP humerus view?
It shows the entire humerus and is used for fracture, alignment and general evaluation.

### 4. What is the purpose of lateral humerus view?
It shows the humerus in side view and helps to assess displacement and fracture line.

### 5. Why is horizontal beam lateral used in trauma?
Because the patient cannot move the arm and we need to avoid pain and additional injury.

### 6. What is the difference between AP and lateral humerus?
AP is front-to-back view and lateral is side view; in AP epicondyles are parallel and in lateral they are superimposed.

---

## 11. 5-mark answer pattern (very useful)

### Example answer for 5 marks

“Humerus is the long bone of the upper arm extending from the shoulder to the elbow. The important landmarks are the head, greater tubercle, lesser tubercle, surgical neck, shaft, deltoid tuberosity, medial and lateral epicondyles, trochlea and capitulum. The AP humerus view is taken with the patient erect or supine, arm extended, and epicondyles parallel to the cassette. The central ray is directed to the midshaft of the humerus. The lateral humerus view is taken with the elbow flexed and epicondyles superimposed to show side view of the shaft and elbow joint. In trauma, horizontal beam lateral projection is used when the arm cannot be rotated or moved. This projection helps in identifying proximal humerus fractures without causing further pain or displacement.”

---

## 12. Final exam-ready summary

- Humerus = upper arm bone
- Proximal end = head, neck, tubercles
- Shaft = long middle part
- Distal end = epicondyles, trochlea, capitulum
- AP humerus = routine general view
- Lateral humerus = side view
- Horizontal beam lateral = trauma view
- Surgical neck = common fracture site
- Always include shoulder and elbow in the image

---

## 13. Very short one-line revision

“Humerus is the long bone of the upper arm; AP and lateral views are basic projections, while horizontal beam lateral is the trauma view, and the surgical neck is the common fracture site.”

This is enough for a good short answer in your exam.
