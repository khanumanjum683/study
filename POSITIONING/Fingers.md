# Fingers Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Finger radiography is a common topic in radiographic positioning. The main views are:
- PA finger
- Oblique finger
- Lateral finger

This topic is usually asked in short answer or short essay questions.

For every view, always include:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Central ray (CR)
- Structures shown

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of fingers
The finger is made of:
- Distal phalanx
- Middle phalanx
- Proximal phalanx
- Metacarpal head
- Interphalangeal joints
- Metacarpophalangeal joint

There are 5 digits:
- Thumb
- Index finger
- Middle finger
- Ring finger
- Little finger

Important point:
In finger radiography, only one finger is usually examined at a time, unless both hands are asked for comparison.

---

## 3. PA finger view

### Indication
- Finger trauma
- Fracture
- Dislocation
- Infection
- Foreign body
- Tumor or deformity

### Position of patient
- Patient sits beside the X-ray table
- Affected finger is placed flat on the cassette
- Palm is resting on the cassette
- Finger is extended and separated from other fingers

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the affected finger
- Centered to the proximal interphalangeal joint or metacarpophalangeal joint depending on the area being examined

### Structures shown
- Distal phalanx
- Middle phalanx
- Proximal phalanx
- Metacarpal head
- IP joints and MCP joint

### Key points
- This is the routine projection for the finger
- Best for general evaluation of finger bones
- Useful to see fracture, dislocation and soft tissue swelling

### Diagram
```text
X-ray tube
    |
    V
Affected finger flat on cassette
Palm touching film
Finger extended
   Distal phalanx
   Middle phalanx
   Proximal phalanx
```

### Exam answer
"PA finger view is the routine projection used for finger trauma and pathology. The patient is positioned with the affected finger placed flat on the cassette and the palm resting on the film. The finger is extended and separated from the others. The central ray is directed to the affected finger and this view shows the distal, middle and proximal phalanges and the joints of the finger."

---

## 4. Oblique finger view

### Indication
- Fracture not seen in PA view
- Better view of the base and shaft of finger
- Joint pathology
- Small bone lesion

### Position of patient
- Affected finger is rotated 45° laterally or medially
- Palm remains on the cassette
- Finger is slightly separated from adjacent fingers

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the affected finger
- Centered to the affected joint

### Structures shown
- Finger bones in oblique position
- Better view of the metacarpal head and joints
- Less overlap than PA view

### Key points
- This view helps in better visualization of the finger bones
- It reduces superimposition of adjacent structures

### Diagram
```text
Oblique finger view
Affected finger rotated 45°
Palm on cassette
Less overlap of bones
Better view of joints
```

### Exam answer
"Oblique finger view is taken by rotating the affected finger 45° so that the finger is seen in oblique position. This reduces overlap and helps to visualize the fractures and joints more clearly. The central ray is directed to the affected finger and the view shows the phalanges and metacarpal head with better separation."

---

## 5. Lateral finger view

### Indication
- Fracture displacement
- Joint alignment
- Angular deformity
- Foreign body in soft tissue

### Position of patient
- Affected finger is placed in true lateral position
- Finger is separated from other fingers
- Thumb side may be placed depending on the view required

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the finger
- Center at the phalangeal region

### Structures shown
- Phalanges in true lateral position
- Interphalangeal joints
- Metacarpophalangeal joint
- Fracture displacement and alignment

### Key points
- Lateral finger view is very important in fractures
- It helps to assess displacement, shortening and angular deformity

### Diagram
```text
X-ray tube
    |
    V
Finger in true lateral position
Separated from other fingers
Shows fracture alignment and joint relation
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral finger view is used to assess the alignment of the finger bones and detect fracture displacement. The affected finger is placed in true lateral position and separated from the other fingers. The central ray is directed to the finger and the view shows the phalanges and joints clearly in lateral relationship."

---

## 6. Comparison table

| View | Main purpose | Important point |
|---|---|---|
| PA finger | Routine study | Shows finger bones and joints |
| Oblique finger | Better detail | Reduces overlap |
| Lateral finger | Alignment and fracture displacement | Best for checking deformity |

---

## 7. Very short exam notes
- PA finger = routine projection
- Oblique = 45° rotation, better visibility
- Lateral = fracture alignment and displacement

---

## 8. Final easy 5-mark answer
"Finger radiography is done mainly in PA, oblique and lateral views. In PA finger view, the affected finger is placed flat on the cassette with the palm resting on the film and the finger extended. This view shows the distal, middle and proximal phalanges and the joints. The oblique view is taken by rotating the finger 45° to reduce overlap and better visualize fractures. The lateral view is used to assess fracture displacement and alignment of the finger bones. These views are important for detecting trauma, dislocation and joint pathology."

---

## 9. Memory trick
- PA = palm down, finger straight
- Oblique = finger rotated 45°
- Lateral = finger side view

This topic is easy and scoring if you remember the position and purpose of each view.
