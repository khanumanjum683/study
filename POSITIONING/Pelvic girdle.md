# Pelvic Girdle and Proximal Femur Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Pelvic girdle and proximal femur are important topics in radiographic positioning. The common projections are:
- AP pelvis
- Frog lateral (modified cleaves method)
- AP axial for pelvic outlet (Taylor method)
- AP axial for pelvic inlet (modified linfield method)
- Posterior oblique - acetabulum (Judet method)
- Hip and proximal femur
  - AP unilateral hip
  - Axiolateral, inferosuperior (Danelius - Miller method)
  - Unilateral frog leg (modified cleaves method)
  - Modified axiolateral (clements-nakayama method)
  - Sacroiliac joints: AP, posterior obliques

This topic is often asked in long essay and short essay questions.

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy
Pelvic girdle consists of:
- Two hip bones
- Sacrum
- Coccyx

Each hip bone is formed by:
- Ilium
- Ischium
- Pubis

Important bony landmarks:
- Iliac wing
- ASIS (anterior superior iliac spine)
- PSIS (posterior superior iliac spine)
- Greater trochanter
- Lesser trochanter
- Pubic symphysis
- Acetabulum
- Sacroiliac joint

Important joints:
- Hip joint
- Sacroiliac joint
- Symphysis pubis

## 3. AP pelvis

### Indication
- Hip pain
- Trauma to pelvis or hip
- Fracture
- Infection
- Joint disease
- Tumor

### Patient position
- Patient is supine
- Both feet are internally rotated 15° to 20°
- Knees are extended
- Midline of the body is centered to the cassette
- Symphysis pubis and iliac wings should be included

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the midline of the pelvis
- Centered midway between the anterior superior iliac spines and symphysis pubis

### Structures shown
- Both hip bones
- Sacrum and coccyx
- Symphysis pubis
- Acetabula
- Femoral heads and necks
- Greater trochanters

### Key points
- AP pelvis is the routine projection
- Internal rotation of the feet helps to place the femoral necks correctly
- It shows both hips in one film

### Simple diagram
```text
       X-ray tube
           |
           V
   Patient supine
   Feet internally rotated 15°-20°
  Both hips included
    Iliac wing  Symphysis pubis
       Femoral heads and necks
           AP Pelvis view
```

### Exam answer
"AP pelvis is the routine projection to evaluate the pelvic girdle, hip joints and proximal femora. The patient is placed supine with both feet internally rotated 15° to 20° to place the femoral necks parallel to the film. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the pelvis at a point midway between the ASIS and symphysis pubis. This view shows both hip bones, acetabula, femoral heads, necks, greater trochanters and sacrum-coccyx region clearly."

## 4. Frog lateral (modified cleaves method)

### Indication
- Hip pathology
- Femoral neck fracture
- Dislocation
- Osteoarthritis
- Bone lesions

### Patient position
- Patient lies supine
- Hip is flexed and abducted
- Knee is flexed
- The leg is rotated outward to create a frog-like position
- The affected side is centered to the cassette

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the femoral neck
- Centered to the hip joint

### Structures shown
- Femoral neck
- Greater trochanter
- Lesser trochanter
- Acetabulum
- Hip joint

### Key points
- This view is mainly for the femoral neck and trochanteric region
- It is used to assess fracture and femoral neck alignment
- The femoral neck should be seen without overlap

### Simple diagram
```text
      Frog leg position
      Hip flexed and abducted
      Knee flexed
      Femoral neck shown clearly
        Frog lateral hip view
```

### Exam answer
"Frog lateral view is taken to assess the femoral neck and trochanteric region. The patient is supine with the hip flexed and abducted and the knee flexed, giving a frog-like position. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the femoral neck. This view shows the femoral neck, greater trochanter, acetabulum and hip joint clearly and is useful in detecting fracture or dislocation."

## 5. AP axial for pelvic outlet (Taylor method)

### Indication
- Pelvic outlet assessment
- Pubic symphysis and ischial region evaluation
- Pelvic trauma
- Craniocaudal deformity

### Patient position
- Patient is supine
- Pelvis is centered to the cassette
- The patient is kept in AP position

### Central ray
- Directed cephalad or caudad depending on the method
- Usually 35° to 45° cephalad for outlet view
- Centered to the inferior margin of the pubic symphysis

### Structures shown
- Pubic symphysis
- Ischial spines
- Pelvic outlet region
- Sacrum and coccyx

### Key points
- Outlet view shows the pelvic outlet clearly
- Useful in assessing pubic symphysis and ischial region
- Helps in pelvic deformity and fracture evaluation

### Simple diagram
```text
    Patient supine
     AP pelvis
    Beam angled cephalad
     Pelvic outlet seen clearly
     AP axial outlet view
```

### Exam answer
"AP axial pelvic outlet view is used to study the pelvic outlet. The patient is placed supine in AP position with the pelvis centered to the cassette. The central ray is angled cephalad, usually 35° to 45°, and directed towards the inferior aspect of the pubic symphysis. This projection shows the pubic symphysis, ischial region and pelvic outlet clearly."

---

## 6. AP axial for pelvic inlet (modified linfield method)

### Indication
- Pelvic inlet study
- Pelvic deformity
- Trauma
- Oblique pelvic assessment

### Patient position
- Patient is supine
- AP position maintained
- Pelvis carefully centered

### Central ray
- Directed caudad
- Centered to the level of the ASIS or pubic symphysis

### Structures shown
- Pelvic inlet
- Sacrum
- Pubic symphysis
- True pelvis region

### Key points
- Inlet view provides a clear view of the true pelvic cavity
- Used to assess pelvic dimensions and deformity

### Exam answer
"AP axial pelvic inlet view is used to demonstrate the pelvic inlet and true pelvis. The patient is positioned supine in AP projection. The central ray is directed caudad and centered to the pubic symphysis or ASIS region. This view is useful for evaluating pelvic dimensions and deformity."

---

## 7. Posterior oblique acetabulum (Judet method)

### Indication
- Acetabular fracture
- Posterior dislocation
- Acetabular pathology
- Hip trauma

### Patient position
- Patient is in posterior oblique position
- The affected side is rotated 45°
- Patient is lying prone or semisupine depending on method

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the affected acetabulum
- Centered to the acetabulum

### Structures shown
- Acetabulum
- Posterior or anterior column of acetabulum
- Femoral head
- Hip joint

### Key points
- This view is very important for acetabular fracture assessment
- It helps to see the acetabulum in oblique view
- Different oblique positions show different acetabular parts

### Exam answer
"Posterior oblique acetabulum view is used to demonstrate the acetabulum and detect fractures. The patient is rotated into a posterior oblique position with the affected side 45° away from the film. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the acetabulum. This projection shows the acetabular rim and hip joint clearly and is helpful in evaluating acetabular fractures and dislocations."

---

## 8. Hip and proximal femur: AP unilateral hip

### Indication
- Hip pain
- Trauma
- Fracture neck of femur
- Arthritis
- Bone pathology

### Patient position
- Patient is supine
- Affected limb is internally rotated 15° to 20°
- Heel and knee of the affected side are kept in same position
- Pelvis is not rotated

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the femoral neck
- Centered to the femoral neck region

### Structures shown
- Femoral head
- Femoral neck
- Greater trochanter
- Acetabulum
- Proximal femur

### Key points
- Best for hip joint and proximal femur
- Internal rotation helps in seeing the neck without foreshortening

### Exam answer
"AP unilateral hip view is taken to evaluate the hip and proximal femur. The patient is supine with the affected limb internally rotated 15° to 20°. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the femoral neck. This view shows the femoral head, neck, greater trochanter, acetabulum and proximal femur clearly."

---

## 9. Axiolateral, inferosuperior (Danelius-Miller method)

### Indication
- Fracture neck of femur
- Hip dislocation
- Pelvic trauma
- Femoral neck assessment

### Patient position
- Patient lies supine
- Affected leg is elevated or abducted
- The cassette is placed against the lateral side of the thigh
- The leg is externally rotated and supported

### Central ray
- Horizontal beam directed to the femoral neck
- Perpendicular to the long axis of the femoral neck

### Structures shown
- Femoral neck
- Greater trochanter
- Acetabulum
- Proximal femur

### Key points
- This is a very important view for fracture neck of femur
- Used when true AP hip cannot be done
- Horizontal beam reduces overlap of anatomy

### Exam answer
"Axiolateral inferosuperior view is used for fracture neck of femur and other hip injuries. The patient is supine with the affected leg slightly elevated and the cassette placed against the lateral side of the thigh. A horizontal central ray is directed to the femoral neck. This view shows the femoral neck, greater trochanter and proximal femur clearly without overlapping structures."

---

## 10. Unilateral frog leg view (modified cleaves method)

### Indication
- Hip and femoral neck pathology
- Trauma
- Osteoarthritis

### Patient position
- Supine
- Affected hip flexed and abducted
- Knee flexed
- Foot is turned outward

### Central ray
- Perpendicular to the femoral neck

### Structures shown
- Femoral neck
- Greater trochanter
- Lesser trochanter
- Acetabulum

### Key points
- Useful for femoral neck fracture and deformity assessment
- View is good to see the neck and trochanters

---

## 11. Modified axiolateral (Clements-Nakayama method)

### Indication
- Hip fracture in patients who cannot move the limb
- Trauma with limited positioning

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or lateral depending on condition
- Affected hip is placed in modified position with limited movement

### Central ray
- Angled horizontal beam directed to the femoral neck

### Structures shown
- Femoral neck
- Proximal femur
- Acetabulum

### Key points
- This is a modified position for patients who cannot do standard methods
- Helpful in difficult conditions and trauma patients

---

## 12. Sacroiliac joints: AP and posterior oblique views

### AP sacroiliac joint view
- Supine position
- Central ray directed 30° caudad to the level of ASIS
- Shows sacroiliac joints

### Posterior oblique SI joint view
- Patient is in 25° to 30° posterior oblique position
- Affected side is rotated posteriorly
- Shows SI joint space clearly

### Indication
- SI joint pain
- Sacroiliac arthritis
- Trauma
- Inflammatory disease

### Key points
- SI joints are small but important joints
- AP and oblique views help in diagnosis of SI joint pathology

## 14. Very short exam notes
- AP pelvis: both hips, internal rotation of feet, shows pelvis and hip joints
- Frog lateral: femoral neck fracture and trochanteric region
- Outlet view: pubic symphysis and ischial region
- Inlet view: true pelvis and inlet
- Acetabulum oblique: acetabular fracture
- Unilateral hip: femoral neck and proximal femur
- Danelius-Miller: important for fracture neck of femur
- SI joint: AP and oblique views for SI pain

---

## 15. Easy 5-mark answer
"Pelvic girdle and proximal femur radiography includes AP pelvis, frog lateral, pelvic inlet/outlet views, acetabular oblique and hip views. AP pelvis is done with the patient supine and both feet internally rotated 15° to 20°. The central ray is directed to the mid-pelvis. This view shows both hips, acetabula and proximal femora. Frog lateral view is used to assess the femoral neck, as the hip is flexed and abducted. AP axial outlet view shows the pelvic outlet and pubic symphysis, while inlet view shows the true pelvis. Acetabular oblique view is used to assess the acetabulum. Hip views are important to evaluate femoral neck fracture and acetabular pathology."

---

## 16. Final revision summary
Pelvic girdle and proximal femur are very important in exam questions. AP pelvis is the routine projection. Frog lateral and Danelius-Miller views are best for femoral neck, while outlet and inlet views are used for pelvic dimensions and deformity. Posterior oblique acetabulum view is important for acetabular fracture. If you keep these important views in mind, you can answer questions well in the exam.
