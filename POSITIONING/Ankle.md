# Ankle Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Ankle radiography is an important lower limb topic. The main views are:
- AP ankle
- AP mortise view (15° oblique)
- Lateral ankle
- AP stress view

For all ankle views, always mention:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Central ray (CR)
- Structures shown
- Short exam answer

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of ankle
The ankle joint is formed by:
- Distal tibia
- Distal fibula
- Talus
- Medial malleolus
- Lateral malleolus
- Tibiofibular syndesmosis
- Articular surfaces

Important structures seen on ankle radiographs:
- Tibia
- Fibula
- Talus
- Calcaneus
- Medial malleolus
- Lateral malleolus
- Ankle joint space

The ankle joint is important for:
- weight bearing
- mobility during walking
- fractures and dislocation
- ligament injuries

## 3. Ankle: AP view

### Indication
- Ankle trauma
- Fracture of tibia or fibula
- Pain in ankle joint
- Osteoarthritis
- Infection or bone pathology

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or seated
- Leg is extended
- Foot is placed in AP position
- Toe is pointing upward
- Ankle joint is included in the film

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the ankle joint
- Centered midway between malleoli

### Structures shown
- Distal tibia
- Distal fibula
- Talus
- Ankle joint space
- Calcaneus partly seen

### Key points
- AP ankle gives general view of the ankle joint
- It is used to see fractures and bone alignment
- The joint space should be clearly seen

### Simple diagram
```text
       X-ray tube
            |
            V
   Foot straight and extended
      Tibia   Fibula
         |      |
       Ankle joint
         Talus
      AP Ankle view
```

### Exam answer
"AP ankle view is taken to evaluate fractures and bone pathology of the ankle. The patient is positioned with the leg extended and the foot in AP position. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the ankle joint, midway between the malleoli. This view shows the distal tibia, distal fibula, talus and ankle joint space clearly."

## 4. Ankle: AP mortise view (15° oblique)

### Indication
- Suspected ligament injury
- Fracture of malleoli
- Joint space evaluation
- Ankle instability

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or seated
- Leg is extended
- Foot is rotated internally by 15° to 20°
- The lower leg and foot are kept in one plane
- Malleoli should appear symmetric

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the ankle joint
- Centered midway between malleoli

### Structures shown
- Distal tibia and fibula
- Talus
- Mortise joint space
- Malleolar outlines
- Tibiofibular articulation

### Key points
- Mortise view is very important for ankle joint evaluation
- It shows the joint space clearly
- It helps to assess fracture and ligament injury

### Simple diagram
```text
      Foot rotated 15° inward
      Tibia and fibula in line
          Medial malleolus
          Lateral malleolus
             Talus
       Mortise joint space
       AP Mortise view
```

### Exam answer
"AP mortise view is taken by rotating the foot inward by 15° to 20° so that the malleoli are seen in profile. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the ankle joint at the level of the malleoli. This view shows the ankle mortise, joint space, talus, medial and lateral malleoli and tibiofibular articulation clearly. It is valuable in detecting fractures and ligament injuries."

---

## 5. Ankle: Lateral view

### Indication
- Fracture of talus, fibula or tibia
- Ankle pain
- Swelling
- Dislocation
- Soft tissue injury

### Patient position
- Patient lies on the affected side
- Ankle is placed in true lateral position
- Knee is flexed slightly
- Foot is dorsiflexed
- Fibula and tibia should be superimposed

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the ankle joint
- Centered to the medial malleolus

### Structures shown
- Tibia
- Fibula
- Talus
- Calcaneus
- Ankle joint
- Soft tissue swelling

### Key points
- Lateral view is very useful for fracture and displacement assessment
- It helps to see joint alignment and soft tissue swelling
- The position is important for correct interpretation

### Simple diagram
```text
        Patient on affected side
            Foot dorsiflexed
        Tibia and fibula overlap
             Talus
          Calcaneus
       Lateral Ankle view
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral ankle view is taken to assess fracture, displacement and soft tissue swelling. The patient is placed on the affected side with the foot dorsiflexed and the ankle in true lateral position. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the ankle joint and centered to the medial malleolus. This view shows the tibia, fibula, talus and calcaneus in lateral relationship."

---

## 6. Ankle: AP stress view

### Indication
- Ligament injury
- Ankle instability
- Suspected torn ligament
- Recurrent ankle sprain

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or seated
- Foot is placed in AP position
- Stress is applied to the ankle joint by pulling the foot in inversion or eversion
- A physician or radiographer helps apply stress carefully

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the ankle joint
- Centered midway between malleoli

### Structures shown
- Joint space widening
- Ligament tear or instability
- Ankle mortise alignment

### Key points
- This view is done to check ligament tear
- It is useful when plain films are normal but instability is suspected
- Stress view demonstrates abnormal opening of joint space

### Simple diagram
```text
      AP ankle with stress
          Foot pulled by force
       Joint space widened if ligament torn
     Stress view for ankle instability
```

### Exam answer
"AP stress view is done to evaluate ligament injuries and ankle instability. The patient is positioned in AP ankle view and stress is applied by pulling the foot in inversion or eversion. The central ray is directed to the ankle joint. If the ligament is torn, the joint space widens and abnormal movement is seen. This view is helpful when plain radiographs are normal but ligament injury is suspected."

## 7. Comparison table: Ankle projections

| View | Main position | Main use | Important structures seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| AP ankle | Leg extended, foot straight | General fracture and pathology | Distal tibia, fibula, talus, ankle joint |
| AP mortise | Foot internally rotated 15°-20° | Joint space and ligament assessment | Mortise, malleoli, talus, tibiofibular articulation |
| Lateral ankle | Side lying, true lateral | Fracture displacement, soft tissue | Tibia, fibula, talus, calcaneus |
| AP stress | AP position with stress applied | Ligament injury, instability | Joint space widening, abnormal motion |

## 8. Very short exam notes

### AP ankle
- Foot straight
- Leg extended
- Central ray at ankle joint
- Used for fracture and bone pathology

### AP mortise
- Foot rotated 15° inward
- Shows ankle mortise clearly
- Best for joint space and ligament injury

### Lateral ankle
- Side lying
- Foot dorsiflexed
- Checks displacement and soft tissue swelling

### AP stress
- Stress applied to foot
- Checks ligament tear and instability
- Joint space widening is important sign

## 9. Easy 5-mark answer
"The ankle is examined in AP, mortise, lateral and stress views. In AP ankle, the patient is positioned with the leg extended and the foot in AP position. The central ray is directed to the ankle joint midway between the malleoli. This view shows the distal tibia, distal fibula, talus and ankle joint space. In mortise view, the foot is rotated inward by 15° to 20° to visualize the ankle joint clearly. This view is useful for joint space and malleolar fractures. Lateral ankle view is taken with the patient on the affected side and the foot dorsiflexed. It helps in assessing fracture displacement and soft tissue swelling. Stress view is used when ligament injury is suspected; stress applied to the ankle may widen the joint space if the ligaments are torn."

---

## 10. One-line memory trick
- AP = foot straight
- Mortise = foot turned inward 15°
- Lateral = side lying
- Stress = ankle under force to check ligament injury

---

## 11. Final revision summary
Ankle radiography is an important topic in radiographic positioning. The AP view is used for routine ankle evaluation, the mortise view is best for ankle joint space and malleoli, lateral view is for fracture and soft tissue assessment, and stress view is used to diagnose ligament tears and instability. Remember the patient position, central ray, and main structures shown for each view. If you mention these points clearly in the exam, you can score well.
