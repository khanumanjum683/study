# Wrist Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Wrist radiography is a very important topic in radiographic positioning. The main views are:
- PA wrist
- PA oblique wrist
- Lateral wrist
- PA scaphoid view
- Carpal tunnel view (Gaynor-Hart method)
- Carpal bridge view

For every view, always mention:
- Indication
- Position of patient
- Central ray (CR)
- Structures shown

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of wrist
The wrist is formed by:
- Distal radius
- Distal ulna
- Carpal bones
- Metacarpal bases
- Radiocarpal joint

Main carpal bones:
- Scaphoid
- Lunate
- Triquetrum
- Pisiform
- Trapezium
- Trapezoid
- Capitate
- Hamate

Important point:
The scaphoid is very important because it is commonly fractured. A special scaphoid view is taken if scaphoid fracture is suspected.

---

## 3. PA Wrist View

### Indication
- Wrist pain
- Trauma
- Fracture
- Arthritis
- Carpal bone pathology

### Position of patient
- Patient sits with forearm resting on the table
- Palm is down on the cassette
- Wrist is flat on the film
- Fingers are relaxed
- Elbow is slightly flexed

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the wrist joint
- Centered at the carpal region

### Structures shown
- Distal radius
- Distal ulna
- Carpal bones
- Metacarpal bases
- Radiocarpal joint

### Key points
- This is the routine wrist view
- It gives a general view of the wrist bones
- It is used for initial evaluation of trauma

### Diagram
```text
X-ray tube
    |
    V
Patient forearm on table
Palm down
Wrist on cassette
   Distal radius
   Distal ulna
   Carpal bones
```

### Exam answer
"PA wrist view is the routine projection used for wrist trauma and pathology. The patient is positioned with the forearm resting on the table and palm down on the cassette. The central ray is directed to the carpal region. This view shows the distal radius, distal ulna, carpal bones and metacarpal bases."

---

## 4. PA Oblique Wrist View

### Indication
- Fracture
- Carpal bone pathology
- Joint disease
- Better visualization of certain carpal bones

### Position of patient
- Same as PA wrist
- Wrist is rotated 45° externally
- Forearm remains on the table

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the wrist joint

### Structures shown
- Carpal bones with less overlap
- Better view of scaphoid, trapezium and hamate
- Distal radius and ulna

### Key points
- This view is taken to reduce superimposition of bones
- It helps in identifying fractures that may be missed in PA view

### Diagram
```text
Wrist rotated 45°
      /\
     /  \
    /    \
  hand    cassette
  better carpal separation
```

### Exam answer
"PA oblique wrist view is obtained by rotating the wrist 45° externally. It is used to reduce overlap of carpal bones and to show the fractures or pathology not seen in PA view. The central ray is directed to the wrist joint. This view demonstrates the carpal bones more clearly."

---

## 5. Lateral Wrist View

### Indication
- Fracture
- Dislocation
- Alignment study
- Volar/dorsal displacement

### Position of patient
- Forearm is placed in true lateral position
- Thumb side is turned upward
- Wrist lies on the cassette
- Fingers are relaxed

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the wrist joint

### Structures shown
- Distal radius and ulna
- Carpal bones in lateral relationship
- Distal radioulnar joint
- Bone alignment

### Key points
- This view is very useful for checking displacement and angulation
- It helps to assess alignment of radius and ulna

### Diagram
```text
X-ray tube
    |
    V
Wrist in lateral position
Thumb side up
Radius and ulna seen in lateral relationship
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral wrist view is done to assess bone alignment and fracture displacement. The patient is positioned with the wrist in true lateral position and the thumb side upward. The central ray is directed to the wrist joint. This view shows the distal radius and ulna and their relationship, helping in evaluation of displacement and angulation."

---

## 6. PA Scaphoid View

### Indication
- Suspected scaphoid fracture
- Wrist trauma with normal PA wrist view

### Position of patient
- Wrist is placed in PA position
- Hand is ulnar deviated to bring scaphoid clear
- Sometimes slight angle is used depending on the technique

### Central ray
- Directed to the scaphoid region

### Structures shown
- Scaphoid bone clearly visualized
- Distal radius and carpal bones

### Key points
- Scaphoid fractures are often missed on routine PA view
- Ulnar deviation helps in better visualization of scaphoid

### Diagram
```text
PA Scaphoid View
  Hand in ulnar deviation
    Scaphoid separated from overlap
  Better visualization of scaphoid
```

### Exam answer
"PA scaphoid view is used in suspected scaphoid fracture. The patient is placed in PA position and the hand is ulnar deviated to bring the scaphoid clear from superimposition. The central ray is directed to the scaphoid region. This view clearly shows the scaphoid and helps in diagnosing scaphoid fracture."

---

## 7. Radial Deviation and Ulnar Deviation

### Radial deviation
- Hand bends toward the radial side
- Used to study carpal alignment

### Ulnar deviation
- Hand bends toward the ulnar side
- Commonly used in scaphoid view
- Helps in separating the scaphoid from overlapping bones

### Comparison table

| Position | Movement of hand | Main use |
|---|---|---|
| Radial deviation | Toward radial side | Carpal alignment and study |
| Ulnar deviation | Toward ulnar side | Better viewing of scaphoid |

---

## 8. Carpal Tunnel View (Gaynor-Hart Method)

### Indication
- Carpal tunnel syndrome
- Median nerve compression
- Carpal pathology

### Position of patient
- Forearm is supinated
- Wrist is hyperextended
- Hand is positioned to allow the beam to pass through the carpal tunnel

### Central ray
- Directed through the carpal tunnel region
- Usually angled to visualize the tunnel clearly

### Structures shown
- Hook of hamate
- Pisiform
- Carpal tunnel region
- Median nerve groove area

### Key points
- It is used to evaluate the carpal tunnel and related structures
- This view is very useful in suspected median nerve compression

### Diagram
```text
Carpal Tunnel View (Gaynor-Hart)
Forearm supinated
Wrist hyperextended
Beam directed through carpal tunnel
Shows: pisiform, hamate hook
```

### Exam answer
"Carpal tunnel view is taken to assess the carpal tunnel and related structures. The wrist is hyperextended and the beam is directed through the carpal tunnel region. This view demonstrates the pisiform, hook of hamate and carpal tunnel, which helps in evaluating median nerve compression."

---

## 9. Carpal Bridge View

### Indication
- Dorsal carpal arch evaluation
- Carpal fracture
- Some wrist pathologies

### Position of patient
- Wrist is hyperextended
- Dorsal aspect of hand placed on cassette
- Beam is directed tangentially

### Central ray
- Directed tangentially to the carpal arch

### Structures shown
- Dorsal carpal arch
- Carpal bones

### Key points
- Mainly used to evaluate dorsal carpal anatomy
- Helps in some carpal fractures

### Diagram
```text
Carpal Bridge View
Wrist hyperextended
Beam tangential
Shows dorsal carpal arch
```

---

## 10. Quick revision table

| View | Main purpose | Special position | Important structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| PA wrist | Routine wrist study | Palm down | Distal radius, ulna, carpals |
| PA oblique | Better carpal separation | 45° rotation | Carpals |
| Lateral | Alignment / displacement | True lateral | Radius-ulna relation |
| PA scaphoid | Suspected scaphoid fracture | Ulnar deviation | Scaphoid |
| Carpal tunnel | Median nerve evaluation | Hyperextension | Pisiform, hamate hook |
| Carpal bridge | Dorsal carpal arch | Hyperextension + tangential beam | Carpal arch |

---

## 11. Very short exam notes
- PA wrist = routine projection
- PA oblique = better carpal detail
- Lateral = alignment and displacement
- Scaphoid = ulnar deviation
- Carpal tunnel = hyperextension
- Carpal bridge = tangential beam

---

## 12. Final easy 5-mark answer
"Wrist radiography includes routine PA, oblique and lateral views. In PA wrist, the patient is positioned with the forearm on the table and palm down on the cassette. The central ray is directed to the carpal region and the image shows distal radius, distal ulna and carpal bones. The PA oblique view is obtained by rotating the wrist 45°, while lateral view is used to assess alignment and fracture displacement. Special projections like PA scaphoid, carpal tunnel and carpal bridge are used for suspected scaphoid fracture, carpal tunnel syndrome and dorsal carpal pathology."

---

## 13. Exam memory trick
- PA wrist = palm down
- Oblique = 45° rotation
- Lateral = thumb up
- Scaphoid = ulnar deviation
- Carpal tunnel = hyperextended wrist
- Carpal bridge = tangential beam

This topic is very scoring if you learn the position and purpose of each view.
