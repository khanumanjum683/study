# Abdomen Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Abdomen radiography is an important topic in radiographic positioning. The main basic views are:
- AP supine (KUB)
- PA prone
- Lateral decubitus
- Erect AP
- Dorsal decubitus
- Lateral abdomen
- Acute abdomen: three-way series

For exam writing, always mention:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Central ray (CR)
- Structures shown
- Why the view is taken

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy
The abdomen contains organs like:
- Liver
- Stomach
- Bowel loops
- Kidneys
- Ureters
- Bladder
- Pancreas
- Spleen
- Aorta

Important structures seen on abdominal radiographs:
- Psoas muscles
- Liver shadow
- Diaphragm
- Bowel gas pattern
- Kidneys
- Bladder
- Flank regions

The abdominal X-ray is used to assess:
- Intestinal obstruction
- Free air
- Stones
- Masses
- Foreign body
- Trauma

## 3. AP supine abdomen (KUB)

### Indication
- Abdominal pain
- Intestinal obstruction
- Urinary stones
- Mass
- Trauma
- Foreign body

### Patient position
- Patient is supine
- Arms are placed by the side
- Legs are extended
- The abdomen is centered to the cassette
- No rotation of the body

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered to the iliac crest or mid-abdomen

### Structures shown
- Liver
- Kidneys
- Bladder
- Bowel gas pattern
- Psoas muscles
- Spine and pelvis

### Key points
- KUB is the basic abdominal X-ray
- It is very important for bowel obstruction and stones
- It shows the abdomen from the diaphragm to the pelvic region

### Simple diagram
```text
       X-ray tube
           |
           V
   Patient supine
   Abdomen centered
   Kidneys   Bowel gas   Bladder
          AP Supine (KUB)
```

### Exam answer
"AP supine abdomen or KUB view is the routine projection used to evaluate the abdomen. The patient is placed supine with the abdomen centered to the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the cassette and centered to the iliac crest. This view shows the liver, kidneys, psoas muscles, bowel gas pattern and bladder clearly. It is useful for suspected obstruction, stone disease and abdominal masses."

---

## 4. PA prone abdomen

### Indication
- Bowel gas pattern
- Abdomen in supine contraindicated
- To reduce magnification of kidneys
- To see bowel loops more clearly

### Patient position
- Patient is prone
- Body in true prone position
- Abdomen centered to the cassette

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered to the lower abdomen or umbilical region

### Structures shown
- Bowel gas pattern
- Kidneys
- Spine
- Pelvis

### Key points
- PA view is less common than AP
- It may reduce kidney magnification
- Useful in some special cases

### Exam answer
"PA prone abdomen is used in selected cases to study abdominal structures and bowel gas pattern. The patient is placed in prone position with the abdomen centered to the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the cassette. This view helps in reducing kidney magnification and demonstrates bowel gas pattern and abdominal structures clearly."

---

## 5. Lateral decubitus abdomen

### Indication
- Free intraperitoneal air
- Bowel obstruction
- Abdominal fluid
- Suspicious pneumoperitoneum

### Patient position
- Patient lies on side
- The side depends on location of suspected pathology
- Left lateral decubitus is commonly used
- The patient must remain in this position for several minutes before exposure

### Central ray
- Directed horizontal
- Centered to the abdomen

### Structures shown
- Free air under diaphragm
- Bowel loops
- Fluid level
- Abdomen wall structures

### Key points
- This view is very important for free air detection
- Air rises, so free air is seen above the liver or under diaphragm
- Highly useful in acute abdomen cases

### Simple diagram
```text
         Air rises upward
       Free air seen under diaphragm
   Left lateral decubitus position
        Lateral decubitus abdomen
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral decubitus abdomen is used mainly to detect free intraperitoneal air. The patient lies on the side, commonly left lateral decubitus, and the central ray is directed horizontally through the abdomen. Free air rises and is seen as a radiolucent area under the diaphragm. This view is very important in patients with acute abdomen when upright position is not possible."

---

## 6. Erect AP abdomen

### Indication
- Free intraperitoneal air
- Suspected perforation peritonitis
- Acute abdomen

### Patient position
- Patient stands upright
- Film is placed behind the patient or against the upright bucky
- The abdomen is centered
- The patient should remain erect for 5 to 10 minutes if possible

### Central ray
- Directed horizontal to the cassette
- Centered to the level of the diaphragm

### Structures shown
- Free air under the diaphragm
- Bowel pattern
- Liver border
- Diaphragm

### Key points
- Erect AP abdomen is the best view for free intraperitoneal air
- Free air is seen under the diaphragm
- Used when perforation is suspected

### Exam answer
"Erect AP abdomen is performed when perforation of a hollow viscus is suspected. The patient stands upright and the central ray is directed horizontally to the diaphragm. This view demonstrates free intraperitoneal air under the diaphragm and also shows bowel air pattern and abdominal contents."

---

## 7. Dorsal decubitus abdomen

### Indication
- Patient unable to stand or sit
- Suspected free air
- Acute abdomen

### Patient position
- Patient lies supine on the side
- The body is rotated slightly to allow air to collect
- Left or right lateral position may be used

### Central ray
- Directed horizontal
- Centered to the abdomen

### Structures shown
- Free air
- Bowel loops
- Diaphragm and abdominal contents

### Key points
- This is used when the patient cannot stand for erect view
- It helps to identify free air in the abdomen

### Exam answer
"Dorsal decubitus abdomen is used when the patient cannot stand or sit for an erect film. The patient is placed in a decubitus position and the central ray is directed horizontally. This helps in demonstrating free air and abdominal fluid collections when upright position is not possible."

---

## 8. Lateral abdomen

### Indication
- Abdominal mass
- Foreign body
- Pathology in one side of abdomen
- Trauma

### Patient position
- Patient is lateral
- Affected side can be against the cassette
- Body is aligned properly

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered to the anterior abdominal wall or mid-abdomen

### Structures shown
- Side of abdomen
- Kidneys
- Bowel pattern
- Psoas muscles

### Key points
- Helpful in localizing abdominal masses and pathology
- Better for one-sided abdominal lesions

### Exam answer
"Lateral abdomen is taken for localization of abdominal masses and pathology. The patient is placed in lateral position and the central ray is directed to the abdominal region. This view helps in determining the side and depth of the lesion and is especially useful when AP supine view is not enough."

---

## 9. Acute abdomen: three-way series

### Indication
- Acute abdominal pain
- Suspected perforation
- Intestinal obstruction
- Stones
- Trauma

### Views included
- AP supine abdomen (KUB)
- Erect PA or AP abdomen
- Chest PA or erect chest

### Why this series is done
- To assess bowel obstruction
- To detect free air
- To detect fluid level
- To evaluate abdominal pathology

### Important points
- Three-way series is the common acute abdomen protocol
- It is very important in emergency conditions
- It helps detect perforation and obstruction quickly

### Exam answer
"Three-way acute abdomen series includes AP supine abdomen, erect abdominal film and chest radiograph. This series is used in acute abdominal pain to evaluate intestinal obstruction, perforation and free intraperitoneal air. The supine film shows bowel gas pattern and stones, the erect film shows air under the diaphragm, and the chest film helps to detect free air and lung pathology."

## 10. Comparison table

| View | Main use | Important finding |
|---|---|---|
| AP supine (KUB) | Routine abdomen | Bowel gas, stones, mass |
| PA prone | Special abdomen | Bowel gas, less kidney magnification |
| Lateral decubitus | Free fluid/air | Air-fluid level |
| Erect AP | Perforation | Free air under diaphragm |
| Dorsal decubitus | Unable to stand | Free air |
| Lateral abdomen | Localization | Side pathology |
| Three-way series | Acute abdomen | Obstruction, perforation, air-fluid level |

## 12. Easy 5-mark answer
"Abdominal radiography includes AP supine abdomen, PA prone, lateral decubitus, erect AP and acute abdomen three-way series. AP supine abdomen or KUB is the routine view used for abdominal pain, stones and obstruction. The patient is supine and the central ray is directed to the iliac crest. Erect AP abdomen is taken when perforation is suspected, because it shows free intraperitoneal air under the diaphragm. Lateral decubitus view is used when the patient cannot stand, and it helps to detect free air or fluid. Three-way acute abdomen series includes AP supine abdomen, erect abdomen and chest film, and is very useful in acute abdominal pain, bowel obstruction and perforation."

---

## 13. One-line memory trick
- KUB = routine abdomen
- Erect = free air under diaphragm
- Decubitus = fluid or air level when patient cannot stand
- Three-way series = supine + erect + chest

---

## 14. Final revision summary
Abdomen imaging is important in emergency and routine practice. The most common view is AP supine KUB, while erect and decubitus views are used to detect free intraperitoneal air and fluid. Acute abdomen three-way series is very important in exam questions. Remember patient position, radiation direction and the key findings such as free air under diaphragm, bowel obstruction and stones.
