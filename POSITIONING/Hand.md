# Hand Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Hand radiography is a common topic in radiographic positioning. The important views are:
- PA hand
- PA oblique hand
- Lateral hand
- Lateral flexion and extension views
- AP bilateral oblique hand (Norgaard method)

For every view, exam answers should include:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Central ray (CR)
- Structures shown
- Why the view is taken

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy of hand
The hand is made of:
- Phalanges
- Metacarpals
- Carpals
- Radius and ulna at the wrist
- Interphalangeal joints
- Metacarpophalangeal joints
- Carpometacarpal joints

The hand has 5 digits:
- Thumb
- Index finger
- Middle finger
- Ring finger
- Little finger

Important bones:
- Distal phalanx
- Middle phalanx
- Proximal phalanx
- Metacarpals I to V
- Sesamoid bones near thumb

---

## 3. PA Hand View

### Indication
- Trauma to hand
- Fracture of metacarpals or phalanges
- Foreign body
- Infection
- Joint disease

### Position of patient
- Patient sits with hand placed flat on cassette
- Palm touching the film
- Fingers extended and separated slightly
- Forearm is resting on the table

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the hand
- Centered at the 3rd metacarpophalangeal joint

### Structures shown
- Phalanges
- Metacarpals
- Sesamoid bones of thumb
- Carpals
- Joints of hand

### Key points
- This is the routine view of the hand
- Best for general assessment of bony structures
- Used in fracture and dislocation evaluation

### Diagram
```text
X-ray tube
    |
    V
Patient hand on cassette
Palm down
Fingers extended
      Phalanges
      Metacarpals
      Carpals
```

### Exam answer
"PA hand view is the routine projection used for evaluation of hand trauma and bone pathology. The patient is positioned with the palm placed flat on the cassette and fingers extended. The central ray is centered at the 3rd metacarpophalangeal joint. This view shows the phalanges, metacarpals, carpals and joints of the hand."

---

## 4. PA Oblique Hand View

### Indication
- Fracture
- Metacarpal injury
- Joint pathology
- Better evaluation of some bones not clearly seen in PA view

### Position of patient
- Hand is rotated 45° externally
- Palm rests on the cassette
- Fingers are slightly separated

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the hand
- Centered at the 3rd MCP joint

### Structures shown
- Phalanges and metacarpals are separated more clearly
- Some joints are better seen
- Fracture lines can be seen more clearly

### Key points
- This view reduces overlap of bones
- Very useful for detecting metacarpal and phalangeal fractures

### Diagram
```text
PA Oblique Hand
Hand rotated 45°
Palm on cassette
Bone overlap reduced
Better view of metacarpals
```

### Exam answer
"PA oblique hand view is taken by rotating the hand 45° externally. The central ray is directed to the third MCP joint. This view reduces superimposition of the bones and helps in better visualization of the metacarpals and phalanges, especially in fractures and dislocations."

---

## 5. Lateral Hand View

### Indication
- Fracture displacement
- Joint alignment
- Soft tissue swelling
- Finger deformity

### Position of patient
- Hand is placed in true lateral position
- Thumb side is up
- Fingers are extended and slightly separated
- Ulnar border or radial border depending on technique

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the hand
- Centered at the metacarpal region

### Structures shown
- Phalanges
- Metacarpals
- Thumb and 1st metacarpal
- Interphalangeal and MCP joints

### Key points
- Lateral view is used to assess alignment of bones
- Important in fracture displacement and joint relation

### Diagram
```text
X-ray tube
    |
    V
Hand in true lateral position
Thumb side up
Shows bone alignment and joint relation
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral hand view is done to assess bone alignment and fracture displacement. The patient is positioned with the hand in true lateral position and the thumb side upward. The central ray is directed to the hand region. This view is useful for evaluating the relationship of the metacarpals and phalanges and for checking fracture displacement."

---

## 6. Lateral Flexion and Extension View

This is important in hand trauma and joint mobility evaluation.

### Lateral flexion view
- Hand is flexed laterally
- Used to assess joint movement and deformity

### Lateral extension view
- Hand is extended laterally
- Used to see deformity and bone alignment in extension position

### Indication
- Fracture
- Joint instability
- Ligament injury
- Malalignment

### Key points
- These views help to compare the position of bones during flexion and extension
- Useful for detecting deformity and abnormal movement

### Diagram
```text
Lateral Flexion View        Lateral Extension View
     Hand flexed                     Hand extended
     bone alignment check          bone alignment check
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral flexion and extension views are used to evaluate the alignment and movement of the hand bones. In lateral flexion, the hand is flexed and rotated into a lateral position, whereas in extension view the hand is extended. These views help in detecting deformity, instability and abnormal joint positions."

---

## 7. AP Bilateral Oblique Hand View (Norgaard Method)

This is a very important special view.

### Indication
- Metacarpal head fracture
- Joint disease
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Early inflammatory changes

### Position of patient
- Both hands are placed in AP oblique position
- Hands are pronated and rotated gently outward
- The palms are placed on the film with the fingers relaxed

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the film
- Centered at the metacarpophalangeal joints

### Structures shown
- Metacarpal heads
- MCP joints
- Joint spaces
- Soft tissue outlines

### Key points
- This is also called the Norgaard method
- It is very useful for rheumatoid arthritis and small joint disease
- It shows both hands in one exposure for comparison

### Diagram
```text
AP Bilateral Oblique Hand (Norgaard method)
Both hands in AP oblique position
Palms on cassette
Shows MCP joints and metacarpal heads
```

### Exam answer
"AP bilateral oblique hand view is also called Norgaard method. Both hands are placed in AP oblique position on the cassette, with the palms slightly rotated outward. The central ray is directed to the metacarpophalangeal joints. This view is used mainly for evaluation of metacarpal heads, MCP joints and small joint disease such as rheumatoid arthritis."

---

## 8. Comparison table

| View | Main purpose | Important point |
|---|---|---|
| PA hand | Routine hand study | Shows phalanges and metacarpals |
| PA oblique | Better bone separation | Reduces overlap |
| Lateral | Alignment and fracture displacement | Checks bone relationship |
| Lateral flexion/extension | Joint movement and deformity | Shows mobility and alignment |
| AP bilateral oblique (Norgaard) | MCP joint and rheumatoid evaluation | Both hands in one exposure |

---

## 9. Very short exam notes
- PA hand = routine projection
- PA oblique = better separation of bones
- Lateral = fracture alignment and displacement
- Flexion/extension = mobility and deformity
- Norgaard = both hands, MCP joints, rheumatoid arthritis

---

## 10. Final easy 5-mark answer
"Hand radiography is done in different views depending on the area to be examined. The PA hand view is the routine projection and shows the phalanges, metacarpals, carpals and joints. The PA oblique view is used to reduce overlap of the bones and better visualize fractures. The lateral hand view is used to assess fracture displacement and alignment. Flexion and extension views help in checking deformity and joint movement. The AP bilateral oblique view or Norgaard method is used to evaluate the metacarpal heads and MCP joints, especially in rheumatoid arthritis and small joint disease."

---

## 11. Exam memory trick
- PA hand = palm down
- PA oblique = 45° rotation
- Lateral = thumb up
- Flexion/extension = movement of hand
- Norgaard = both hands, MCP joints

This topic is easy if you remember the purpose of each view and the position of the hand.
