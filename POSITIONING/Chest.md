# Chest Radiography

## 1. Important exam idea
Chest radiography is a very important topic in radiographic positioning. The basic views are:
- PA chest
- Lateral chest

Special chest projections:
- AP supine and semierect
- Lateral decubitus
- AP lordotic
- Anterior oblique
- Posterior oblique
- Upper airway: AP, LAT

The question paper usually asks:
- Indication
- Patient position
- Central ray (CR)
- Structures shown
- Short easy answer

---

## 2. Related radiological anatomy
The chest contains:
- Lungs
- Heart
- Trachea
- Bronchi
- Diaphragm
- Clavicles
- Ribs
- Mediastinum

Important structures in chest X-ray:
- Trachea
- Carina
- Both lung fields
- Diaphragm domes
- Costophrenic angles
- Heart border
- Hilum

In chest radiography, we normally look for:
- Lung field expansion
- Pleural effusion
- Pneumothorax
- Consolidation
- Heart size
- Fracture or foreign body

## 3. PA chest view

### Indication
- Chest pain
- Cough
- Fever
- Tuberculosis
- Lung pathology
- Trauma to chest

### Patient position
- Patient stands upright facing the cassette
- Chest is placed against the cassette
- Nose and chin slightly raised
- Arms are resting on the sides or raised upward
- Shoulders are rolled forward
- Deep inspiration is taken

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered at the level of T7 (midway between jugular notch and xiphoid)

### Structures shown
- Both lungs
- Trachea
- Heart
- Diaphragm
- Costophrenic angles
- Bony thorax

### Key points
- PA chest is the routine chest view
- It gives better heart size and lung field detail
- The patient must take full inspiration

### Simple diagram
```text
        X-ray tube
            |
            V
   Patient stands upright
   Chest against cassette
   Full inspiration
  lungs   heart   diaphragm
          PA Chest view
```

### Exam answer
"PA chest view is the routine projection used for chest evaluation. The patient stands upright facing the cassette with the chest against the film, shoulders rolled forward and deep inspiration taken. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the cassette at the level of T7. This view shows both lungs, trachea, heart, diaphragm and costophrenic angles clearly."

## 4. Lateral chest view

### Indication
- Lung lesion
- Pleural effusion
- Mediastinal mass
- Better localization of disease
- Chest trauma

### Patient position
- Patient stands with side of chest against cassette
- Left lateral is generally used in routine chest radiography
- Arms raised above head
- Body in true lateral position
- Full inspiration taken

### Central ray
- Directed horizontal to the chest
- Centered at the level of T7

### Structures shown
- Both lungs in side profile
- Heart
- Aortic arch
- Diaphragm
- Posterior ribs
- Lesions behind heart or lung bases

### Key points
- Lateral view helps to localize pathology more accurately
- It is very useful for small lesions and pleural effusion
- Left lateral chest is commonly used because heart is nearer to left side

### Simple diagram
```text
        X-ray tube
            |
            V
    Patient side against cassette
     Left lateral position common
     heart  lung  diaphragm
          Lateral chest view
```

### Exam answer
"Lateral chest view is done to localize lesions and to see the lung fields in profile. The patient stands sideways with the chest against the cassette, usually in left lateral position, and takes deep inspiration. The central ray is directed horizontal to the level of T7. This view shows the heart, lungs, diaphragm and mediastinum in profile and helps in localizing chest lesions clearly."

## 5. AP supine and semierect chest

### Indication
- Patient unable to stand
- Ill or weak patient
- ICU patient
- Emergency chest X-ray

### Patient position
- Patient is supine or semierect
- Chest is centered to the cassette
- May be done in bed or stretcher

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered at the level of the sternal angle or T7

### Structures shown
- Lungs
- Heart
- Diaphragm
- Mediastinum

### Key points
- AP supine view is used when patient cannot stand
- Heart appears larger because of magnification
- It is not as good as PA chest for heart size evaluation

### Exam answer
"AP supine or semierect chest view is taken when the patient cannot stand. The patient is supine or semierect with the chest against the cassette. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the cassette. This view shows the lungs and mediastinum but the cardiac silhouette may appear enlarged because of magnification."

---

## 6. Lateral decubitus chest

### Indication
- Pleural effusion
- Pneumothorax
- Air-fluid level
- To assess fluid level in pleural cavity

### Patient position
- Patient lies on one side
- The side with suspected fluid is down for effusion
- The side with suspected air is up for pneumothorax
- Arms raised above head

### Central ray
- Directed horizontal
- Centered to the chest at T7

### Structures shown
- Pleural fluid or air level
- Lung bases
- Diaphragm margin

### Key points
- This is a very useful special chest projection
- Helps to detect small pleural effusion
- Best for air-fluid level demonstration

### Exam answer
"Lateral decubitus chest view is used to detect fluid or air in the pleural space. The patient lies on one side with the suspected side down if pleural effusion is suspected, or up if pneumothorax is suspected. The central ray is directed horizontally to the chest. This view demonstrates air-fluid levels clearly and helps in identifying pleural effusion or pneumothorax."

---

## 7. AP lordotic chest

### Indication
- Upper lobe collapse
- Apical lesion
- Pulmonary pathology in upper lung zones
- To see clavicle and apices better

### Patient position
- Patient stands about 1 foot away from cassette
- Back is against cassette
- The patient leans backward so shoulders and clavicles move upward
- Chin is raised slightly

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered to the sternal angle or clavicle region

### Structures shown
- Apical region of lungs
- Clavicles
- Upper lobes

### Key points
- This view better shows the lung apices
- It clears the clavicles from the lung apices
- Often used for suspected apical lesion

### Exam answer
"AP lordotic view is taken to visualize the apical region of the lungs. The patient stands with the back against the cassette and leans backward, producing a lordotic position. The central ray is directed perpendicular to the cassette. This projection lifts the clavicles and helps in visualizing the upper lobe and apical lung fields clearly."

---

## 8. Anterior and posterior oblique chest views

### Indication
- To study mediastinal structures
- To localize lesions
- Cardiac evaluation
- Lung pathology

### Patient position
- Patient rotates 45° to the cassette
- Anterior oblique: patient faces the cassette and rotates
- Posterior oblique: patient backs away from the cassette and rotates

### Central ray
- Directed perpendicular to the cassette
- Centered at T7

### Structures shown
- Lungs
- Heart shadow in different profile
- Mediastinal structures
- Right or left side structures

### Key points
- Oblique views help to see structures behind the heart and lungs
- They improve localization of lesions

### Exam answer
"Oblique chest views are taken by rotating the patient 45° to the cassette. Anterior oblique and posterior oblique projections help in localizing mediastinal and pulmonary lesions. The central ray is directed to T7. These views show the heart and lung fields in oblique profile and are useful for evaluating hidden lesions."

## 9. Upper airway: AP and lateral views

### Indication
- Upper airway obstruction
- Foreign body in airway
- Epiglottitis
- Croup
- Soft tissue neck mass

### AP upper airway
- Patient is supine or standing
- Face and neck are in AP projection
- Central ray directed to the level of the larynx

### Lateral upper airway
- Patient stands sideways
- Neck extended slightly
- Central ray horizontal to the larynx

### Structures shown
- Pharynx
- Larynx
- Trachea
- Airway patency

### Key points
- Upper airway views are used to detect obstruction or foreign body
- A lateral airway view is very useful in suspected epiglottitis and foreign body aspiration

### Exam answer
"Upper airway radiographs include AP and lateral views. These are taken to evaluate the larynx, pharynx and trachea. In lateral airway view, the patient stands sideways with the neck slightly extended and the central ray is directed horizontally to the larynx. The view shows the airway clearly and is useful for detecting obstruction, foreign body or epiglottic swelling."

## 10. Comparison table: chest projections

| View | Main position | Main use | Key structures |
|---|---|---|---|
| PA chest | Upright, chest against cassette | Routine chest | Lungs, heart, diaphragm |
| Lateral chest | Side against cassette | Localize lesion | Heart, lung profile, mediastinum |
| AP supine | Supine/semi-erect | Bed patient | Lungs, heart, diaphragm |
| Decubitus | Side lying | Effusion/pneumothorax | Air-fluid level |
| AP lordotic | Back against cassette, leaning back | Apices | Upper lobe and clavicle region |
| Oblique chest | 45° rotation | Localize lesion | Lung/mediastinal structures |
| Upper airway | AP or lateral neck | Foreign body/obstruction | Pharynx, larynx, trachea |

- Side lying

- Lean back

- 45° rotation
- Localizes lesions

### Upper airway
- AP/LAT neck
- Foreign body and obstruction

## 12. Easy 5-mark answer
"Chest radiography includes the routine PA and lateral views, and special projections like AP supine, decubitus, lordotic and oblique views. In PA chest, the patient stands upright with the chest against the cassette and takes a deep inspiration. The central ray is directed to T7. This view shows both lung fields, heart, diaphragm and costophrenic angles. Lateral chest is taken with the patient in side position to localize abnormalities of the lungs and mediastinum. Decubitus view is used when pleural fluid or air is suspected, because it shows air-fluid levels clearly. AP lordotic view is used to visualize the upper lung apices and clavicles."

---

## 13. One-line memory trick
- PA = patient facing film, upright, full inspiration
- Lateral = side profile, localizes lesions
- Decubitus = side lying, to see fluid or air
- Lordotic = lean back, apices visible

---

## 14. Final revision summary
Chest radiography is the most common X-ray exam. PA chest is the routine view, lateral chest helps localize disease, and special chest views are used for pleural effusion, apical lesion and airway obstruction. Always mention patient position, central ray, inspiration, and main structures shown. These are the points examiners expect.
